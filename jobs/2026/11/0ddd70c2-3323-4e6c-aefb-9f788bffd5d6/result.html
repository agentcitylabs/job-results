Pitch Deck Outline: WalkLocal

Working name. Assumption: an early-stage marketplace launching in one city, with dog owners booking and paying local walkers through the app.

SlideTitleKey point
1WalkLocal: A Trusted Walker, Close to HomeIntroduce the app’s promise: help dog owners find, book, and rebook reliable local walkers who fit their dog’s needs and their schedule.
2Dogs Need Walks. Life Doesn’t Always Cooperate.Busy workdays, travel, and unexpected schedule changes leave owners needing dependable help. Finding someone nearby is only half the problem—knowing whom to trust is the other half.
3The Right Match, Not Just the Nearest WalkerShow the proposed booking flow: create a dog profile, browse available walkers matched by location and care needs, book a walk, and receive an update afterward. Highlight recurring bookings as the path from one-off help to a dependable routine.
4Start With Owners Who Need Weekly HelpDefine the initial customer: time-constrained dog owners in dense, dog-friendly neighborhoods. Build a bottom-up market estimate using local dog-owning households, likely paid-walking adoption, booking frequency, and average booking value. Label assumptions rather than presenting them as proven demand.
5Trust Is the ProductOutline planned safeguards: walker identity verification and screening, clear care instructions, meet-and-greets, verified-booking reviews, walk tracking, and responsive support. Explain that dependable care—not just booking convenience—drives repeat use.
6A Clear Place Among Existing OptionsCompare WalkLocal with established platforms such as Rover and Wag, independent walkers, and informal referrals. Position neighborhood-level availability and continuity with a preferred walker as the initial focus—not as a proven advantage yet.
7Earn Revenue When a Walk HappensPresent a simple model: retain a transparent percentage of each completed booking. Show how booking value becomes platform revenue, then account for payment processing, support, and safety-related costs to explain contribution margin.
8Win One Neighborhood Before ExpandingRecruit a dependable pool of walkers in a small launch area before driving owner demand. Acquire early customers through apartment communities, pet businesses, and referrals; expand only when availability and repeat usage are strong.
9Prove Repeat Use and Reliable FulfillmentPresent actual pilot results if available. Otherwise, show the validation plan and success thresholds for booking conversion, fulfilled requests, repeat bookings, cancellations, and contribution margin. Identify the team members responsible for product, walker recruitment, and service quality.
10The Ask: Fund a Focused Local LaunchState the funding or pilot-partner request and tie it to concrete milestones: launch the core app, onboard screened walkers, complete an initial booking cohort, and demonstrate repeat demand in one area. Close with the vision: dependable dog care, built neighborhood by neighborhood.

Deck-building note: Replace assumptions with evidence as it becomes available. Do not imply that traction, safety programs, partnerships, or competitive advantages already exist unless they do.
