# Pitch Deck Outline: WalkLocal
*Working name. Assumption: an early-stage marketplace launching in one city, with dog owners booking and paying local walkers through the app.*

| Slide | Title | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **WalkLocal: A Trusted Walker, Close to Home** | Introduce the app’s promise: help dog owners find, book, and rebook reliable local walkers who fit their dog’s needs and their schedule. |
| **2** | **Dogs Need Walks. Life Doesn’t Always Cooperate.** | Busy workdays, travel, and unexpected schedule changes leave owners needing dependable help. Finding someone nearby is only half the problem—knowing whom to trust is the other half. |
| **3** | **The Right Match, Not Just the Nearest Walker** | Show the proposed booking flow: create a dog profile, browse available walkers matched by location and care needs, book a walk, and receive an update afterward. Highlight recurring bookings as the path from one-off help to a dependable routine. |
| **4** | **Start With Owners Who Need Weekly Help** | Define the initial customer: time-constrained dog owners in dense, dog-friendly neighborhoods. Build a bottom-up market estimate using local dog-owning households, likely paid-walking adoption, booking frequency, and average booking value. Label assumptions rather than presenting them as proven demand. |
| **5** | **Trust Is the Product** | Outline planned safeguards: walker identity verification and screening, clear care instructions, meet-and-greets, verified-booking reviews, walk tracking, and responsive support. Explain that dependable care—not just booking convenience—drives repeat use. |
| **6** | **A Clear Place Among Existing Options** | Compare WalkLocal with established platforms such as Rover and Wag, independent walkers, and informal referrals. Position neighborhood-level availability and continuity with a preferred walker as the initial focus—not as a proven advantage yet. |
| **7** | **Earn Revenue When a Walk Happens** | Present a simple model: retain a transparent percentage of each completed booking. Show how booking value becomes platform revenue, then account for payment processing, support, and safety-related costs to explain contribution margin. |
| **8** | **Win One Neighborhood Before Expanding** | Recruit a dependable pool of walkers in a small launch area before driving owner demand. Acquire early customers through apartment communities, pet businesses, and referrals; expand only when availability and repeat usage are strong. |
| **9** | **Prove Repeat Use and Reliable Fulfillment** | Present actual pilot results if available. Otherwise, show the validation plan and success thresholds for booking conversion, fulfilled requests, repeat bookings, cancellations, and contribution margin. Identify the team members responsible for product, walker recruitment, and service quality. |
| **10** | **The Ask: Fund a Focused Local Launch** | State the funding or pilot-partner request and tie it to concrete milestones: launch the core app, onboard screened walkers, complete an initial booking cohort, and demonstrate repeat demand in one area. Close with the vision: dependable dog care, built neighborhood by neighborhood. |

**Deck-building note:** Replace assumptions with evidence as it becomes available. Do not imply that traction, safety programs, partnerships, or competitive advantages already exist unless they do.
