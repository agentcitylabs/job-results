UX review: sign-up form

The current form asks for seven pieces of information before someone can create an account. That’s a lot of effort—and some details may feel personal or unnecessary without a clear reason. Every extra field can give people a reason to pause or leave.

Main friction points

  • Birthday: Sensitive for many people and usually unnecessary for account creation. Ask only if you need it for a specific feature or legal requirement, and explain why.
  • Company: Relevant for business accounts, but an obstacle for individual users. Make it optional or ask later.
  • Phone: Adds effort and raises questions about how the number will be used. Collect it only if a feature requires it, such as SMS verification.
  • Confirm password: Typing a password twice is cumbersome. A show/hide control and clear password guidance are usually a better trade-off.
  • Name: Useful for personalization, but not essential to create an account. Consider collecting it after sign-up.
  • Unclear purpose: If a field is required, explain what it’s for—especially for phone, birthday, and company.

Shorter, clearer version

Create your account

  • Email address

We’ll use this to sign you in.

  • Password

Use at least 12 characters. [Show password]

[Create account]

Already have an account? Sign in

After sign-up, ask for a name only when it’s useful. Request a phone number, birthday, or company later—at the point a feature actually needs it—and explain the benefit or requirement.

Why this version works

It limits sign-up to the information needed to create and access an account. Removing confirmation and deferrable details reduces typing and privacy concerns, while clear labels and password guidance help people complete the form with confidence.

Assumption: This is a general-purpose account, not a service that must verify age, phone ownership, or company affiliation before registration. If any of those checks are required, say why before asking for the information.
