# SOP: New Hire First Day

**Purpose:** Give every new team member a welcoming, organized first day with the access, context, and support they need to begin work confidently.

**Applies to:** All new employees, contractors, and interns joining the design agency.

**Day-one owner:** Operations/Office Manager  
**Hiring manager:** The person responsible for the new hire’s role and onboarding

---

## First-Day Schedule and Responsibilities

### 1. Confirm readiness before arrival

**Owner:** Operations/Office Manager  
**When:** Before the first day

- Confirm the start date, arrival time, location, and dress expectations.
- Send the new hire a welcome email with:
  - First-day schedule
  - Office or video-call details
  - Contact number for their day-one contact
  - Instructions for building access or remote setup
- Prepare the workspace, laptop, monitor, supplies, and welcome materials.
- Confirm required accounts are created or ready for activation.
- Notify the team of the new hire’s start date, role, and pronunciation of their name.

### 2. Welcome and introductions

**Owner:** Operations/Office Manager or assigned buddy  
**When:** Start of day

- Greet the new hire promptly.
- Show them their workspace, kitchen, bathrooms, meeting rooms, and emergency exits.
- Introduce them to the team, including:
  - Founder or agency lead
  - Hiring manager
  - Account/project leads
  - Designers and production staff
  - Finance or administrative contact
- Explain the day’s schedule and identify who to contact with questions.

### 3. Complete employment and administrative setup

**Owner:** Operations/Office Manager  
**When:** First hour

- Complete or confirm required employment paperwork.
- Collect payroll and emergency-contact information securely.
- Review working hours, break expectations, holidays, leave procedures, and timesheet requirements.
- Explain the agency’s communication norms, including expected response times and preferred channels.
- Confirm whether the role is office-based, hybrid, or remote and review any equipment responsibilities.

> **Note:** Store personal and payroll information only in the agency’s approved secure system.

### 4. Set up technology and access

**Owner:** Operations/Office Manager or IT support  
**When:** First morning

Confirm access to the tools needed for the role, such as:

- Email and calendar
- Team chat
- Project-management system
- File storage and agency templates
- Design tools and libraries
- Time tracking and expense systems
- Password manager
- VPN or other security tools, if applicable

The new hire should successfully log in, update passwords where required, enable multi-factor authentication, and know where to get technical help.

### 5. Review the agency and role

**Owner:** Hiring Manager  
**When:** Late morning

Cover:

- Agency history, services, clients, and current priorities
- Team structure and key responsibilities
- The new hire’s role, first-week priorities, and expected outcomes
- Current projects they will support
- How briefs, reviews, approvals, handoffs, and file versioning work
- Quality standards and client confidentiality expectations
- How success will be measured during the first 30–90 days

Provide the new hire with links to the employee handbook, brand guidelines, project templates, and relevant client or project background.

### 6. Introduce the first assignment

**Owner:** Hiring Manager or Project Lead  
**When:** Afternoon

Give the new hire a small, clearly defined starter task. It should:

- Have a named owner and deadline
- Include the relevant brief, files, and examples
- Explain the expected format and level of completion
- Identify who will review the work
- Be achievable without requiring extensive undocumented knowledge

Allow time for the new hire to review the materials and ask questions. Avoid assigning urgent client work before access, context, and support are in place.

### 7. Complete a day-one check-in

**Owner:** Hiring Manager  
**When:** Final 30 minutes

Ask the new hire:

- What was clear?
- What remains confusing?
- Are all required tools and accounts working?
- Do they know who to contact for project, administrative, and technical questions?
- Is anything needed before the next working day?

Review the plan for the rest of the week and schedule a follow-up check-in for the end of week one.

### 8. Close out the day

**Owner:** Operations/Office Manager  
**When:** End of day

- Confirm all paperwork and access tasks are recorded as complete.
- Note any outstanding setup items and assign an owner and deadline.
- Send or share the new hire’s first-week plan.
- Check in with the hiring manager about any concerns or support needed.
- Welcome the new hire again and confirm their start time for the next day.

---

## First-Day Checklist

### Before arrival

- [ ] Start date and arrival details confirmed  
- [ ] Welcome email sent  
- [ ] Workspace and equipment prepared  
- [ ] Required software and accounts prepared  
- [ ] Employment paperwork ready  
- [ ] Team notified  
- [ ] Buddy or day-one contact assigned  
- [ ] First-week schedule prepared  

### Morning

- [ ] New hire greeted on arrival  
- [ ] Office or remote setup explained  
- [ ] Team introductions completed  
- [ ] Employment and payroll information completed securely  
- [ ] Working hours, leave, timesheets, and communication norms reviewed  
- [ ] Email, calendar, chat, file storage, and project tools tested  
- [ ] Password and multi-factor authentication setup completed  

### Afternoon

- [ ] Agency overview completed  
- [ ] Role, responsibilities, and first-week priorities reviewed  
- [ ] Project workflow and file standards explained  
- [ ] Relevant documents and resources shared  
- [ ] Starter assignment provided  
- [ ] Reviewer and deadline confirmed  

### End of day

- [ ] Day-one questions answered  
- [ ] Outstanding access or paperwork issues logged  
- [ ] First-week plan confirmed  
- [ ] End-of-week check-in scheduled  
- [ ] Hiring manager and Operations/Office Manager updated  

**Completion standard:** The new hire has the access, information, contacts, and first assignment needed to begin their second day without avoidable delays.
