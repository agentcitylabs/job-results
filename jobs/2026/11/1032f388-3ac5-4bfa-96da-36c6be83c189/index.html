The work, as delivered

The deliverable contains two meal-kit ads, each with a headline, body line and call to action. Version A leads with balanced eating, while Version B leads with less dinner planning; both use the same body and call to action. It includes a hypothesis for each version and a test setup that changes only the headline. It states an assumption that the service provides ingredients and step-by-step recipes for balanced meals.

  • Version A headline: “Put balanced dinners on the menu.”
  • Version B headline: “Take ‘what’s for dinner?’ off your list.”
  • Shared body describes delivered ingredients and step-by-step recipes.
  • Shared call to action: “Explore the menu”.
  • Test setup splits the same audience evenly and holds other ad elements constant.
  • Compare click-through rates, then check first-order conversions before choosing a winner.

Meal-Kit Ad A/B Test

Assumption: The service provides ingredients and step-by-step recipes designed for balanced meals.

Pillar: Balanced dinners, less planning

Make eating well at home feel manageable on an ordinary weeknight.

Spoke 1 — Version A: Lead with eating well

Headline: Put balanced dinners on the menu.

Body: Ingredients and step-by-step recipes delivered to your door. Pick your meals and get cooking.

Call to action: Explore the menu

Spoke 2 — Version B: Lead with less planning

Headline: Take “what’s for dinner?” off your list.

Body: Ingredients and step-by-step recipes delivered to your door. Pick your meals and get cooking.

Call to action: Explore the menu

Spoke 3 — What A tests

Hypothesis: The promise of balanced dinners will motivate people who want to eat well but need a practical way to follow through.

A stronger click-through rate for A would suggest that the nutrition-focused benefit is the better opening hook.

Spoke 4 — What B tests

Hypothesis: Relief from daily dinner decisions will motivate people more than a direct appeal to balanced eating.

A stronger click-through rate for B would suggest that less planning is the better opening hook.

Test setup: Split the same audience evenly. Keep the image, body, CTA, placement and landing page identical so only the headline changes. Compare click-through rates, then check first-order conversions before choosing a winner.
