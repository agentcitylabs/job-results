# SOP: New Hire First Day

**Purpose:** Give every new team member a welcoming, organised first day and the access, context, and support needed to begin contributing safely.

**Applies to:** All new employees, contractors, and freelancers joining the design agency.

**Primary owner:** Operations or Office Manager  
**Supporting owners:** Hiring Manager, IT/Admin, Buddy, Studio Director

---

## First-Day Schedule and Responsibilities

| Step | Timing | Activity | Owner |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Before arrival | Confirm start time, location or video link, dress guidance, and contact person. Prepare desk, equipment, email account, software access, and welcome materials. | Operations / IT |
| 2 | 9:00 | Welcome the new hire, give a brief office tour, introduce the team, and explain the day’s schedule. | Operations |
| 3 | 9:30 | Complete employment paperwork, emergency contact details, payroll information, and confidentiality or IP agreements. | Operations / HR |
| 4 | 10:15 | Set up and test laptop, email, project-management tools, file storage, messaging, and design software. | IT / Operations |
| 5 | 11:00 | Hold an agency overview: services, clients, team structure, values, working hours, communication norms, and approval processes. | Studio Director |
| 6 | 12:00 | Have an informal lunch or coffee with the team or assigned buddy. | Buddy / Hiring Manager |
| 7 | 13:00 | Review the new hire’s role, current priorities, first-week goals, and how success will be measured. | Hiring Manager |
| 8 | 14:00 | Walk through a live or recent project, including the brief, research, design process, review stages, file structure, and client handoff. | Hiring Manager / Project Lead |
| 9 | 15:00 | Complete a small, low-risk starter task or shadow a team member. | Hiring Manager / Buddy |
| 10 | 16:00 | Ask for questions, review any setup issues, and confirm the schedule for Day 2 and the first week. | Hiring Manager |
| 11 | 16:30 | Record completion of onboarding tasks and note any follow-ups. | Operations |

*Times may be adjusted for the role or start date. Avoid filling the entire day with meetings; allow time for settling in and questions.*

## Procedure

### 1. Prepare before the start date

Operations confirms that the following are ready:

- Employment or contractor paperwork
- Laptop, monitor, accessories, and workspace
- Email and calendar account
- Messaging and project-management accounts
- File-storage and document permissions
- Required design software and licenses
- Access to relevant templates, brand guidelines, and project folders
- First-day schedule and contact details
- Named buddy and hiring manager

If any access is delayed, provide a clear workaround and an expected resolution time.

### 2. Welcome and orient the new hire

The Operations owner or designated host should:

1. Meet the new hire on arrival or join the video call promptly.
2. Explain the day’s schedule and where to ask for help.
3. Give a short tour of the office, or an equivalent virtual orientation.
4. Introduce key team members and explain what each person does.
5. Show practical facilities and procedures, including entry, breaks, supplies, and emergency arrangements.

Keep the welcome friendly and low-pressure. The goal is familiarity, not information overload.

### 3. Complete essential administration

Operations or HR collects and confirms:

- Signed contract and confidentiality agreements
- Payroll or invoicing details
- Tax and eligibility documentation, where applicable
- Emergency contact information
- Preferred name and pronouns
- Equipment issue record
- Required policies and acknowledgements

Store personal information only in the approved HR or operations system.

### 4. Set up tools and access

IT or Operations helps the new hire sign in and test:

- Email and calendar
- Team messaging
- Project-management system
- File storage and folder permissions
- Video-conferencing tools
- Design and prototyping software
- Time tracking or project reporting tools
- Password manager and multi-factor authentication

The new hire should successfully open at least one relevant project folder and send a test message before setup is marked complete.

### 5. Explain how the agency works

The Studio Director or delegate covers:

- The agency’s services and typical client types
- Team structure and reporting lines
- How work moves from brief to delivery
- Who approves creative work
- How feedback is given and recorded
- Client communication boundaries
- File naming, version control, and storage expectations
- Meeting, availability, and response-time norms
- How to raise risks, workload concerns, or mistakes

Use real examples where possible. Link to written policies instead of trying to explain every detail verbally.

### 6. Set role expectations

The Hiring Manager reviews:

- The new hire’s responsibilities
- Current projects and relevant background
- First-week priorities
- Initial deliverables
- Working hours and availability
- How progress will be reviewed
- Questions or support needs

Assign one small starter task that is useful, clearly defined, and achievable without extensive context.

### 7. Close the day

Before the new hire finishes, the Hiring Manager should:

- Ask what is clear and what remains confusing
- Confirm all urgent access or equipment issues
- Review the next day’s schedule
- Confirm the first-week check-in
- Share links to essential documents and resources
- Thank the new hire and reinforce how to get help

Operations records incomplete items with an owner and due date.

## First-Day Checklist

### Before arrival

- [ ] Start date and arrival details confirmed
- [ ] Welcome contact assigned
- [ ] Desk or remote setup prepared
- [ ] Laptop and equipment ready
- [ ] Email and core accounts created
- [ ] Software licenses assigned
- [ ] File and project permissions granted
- [ ] Welcome schedule shared
- [ ] Buddy assigned
- [ ] First-week priorities prepared

### During the day

- [ ] New hire welcomed and introduced
- [ ] Office or virtual workspace tour completed
- [ ] Employment and confidentiality paperwork completed
- [ ] Emergency and payroll details collected
- [ ] Equipment issued and tested
- [ ] Email, messaging, files, and project tools tested
- [ ] Agency overview completed
- [ ] Communication and feedback norms explained
- [ ] Role and first-week expectations reviewed
- [ ] Live or recent project walkthrough completed
- [ ] Starter task assigned
- [ ] Questions and concerns discussed

### Before close of day

- [ ] Outstanding access issues logged
- [ ] Owner assigned for each follow-up
- [ ] Day 2 schedule confirmed
- [ ] First-week check-in scheduled
- [ ] New hire knows where to find key documents
- [ ] Onboarding record updated

---

## Completion Standard

The first day is complete when the new hire:

1. Knows who to contact for operational, technical, and role-specific help.
2. Can access the tools and files needed for their role.
3. Understands the agency’s basic workflow and communication norms.
4. Has a clear first-week priority.
5. Knows what will happen next and when their first check-in will occur.
