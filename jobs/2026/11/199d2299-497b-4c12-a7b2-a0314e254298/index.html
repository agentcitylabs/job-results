The work, as delivered

The deliverable contains an initial outreach email, a practical follow-up, and a final close-the-loop email for small-clinic decision-makers. Each email includes three subject line options, a body under 120 words, and send timing. Notes cover personalization, sender signatures, and assumed tool features that should be removed or adjusted if unavailable.

  • Send on Day 1, follow up 4 business days later, then again 6–7 business days later.
  • Messaging focuses on front-desk scheduling work, missed appointments, and phone tag.
  • The emails describe online booking, confirmations, and rescheduling as assumed tool features.
  • Calls to action include a quick look, a 15-minute conversation, and a referral to the scheduling decision-maker.
  • Bracketed fields support personalization with the recipient, clinic, tool, and sender names.

Summary

This is a three-email cold sequence for owners and practice managers at small clinics.

It focuses on reducing front-desk scheduling work, missed appointments, and phone tag.

Send Email 1 first, then follow up briefly if there is no reply. Personalize the bracketed fields before sending.

Email 1 — Initial outreach

Timing: Day 1

Subject line options:

  • A simpler way to handle [Clinic Name]’s appointments
  • Quick idea for your front desk
  • Reducing scheduling back-and-forth at [Clinic Name]

Body:

Hi [First Name],

Running a small clinic often means your front desk is answering calls, chasing confirmations, and finding new times when patients need to reschedule.

[Tool] gives patients a simple way to book, confirm, or move appointments online—while keeping your team’s calendar organized.

That can mean fewer scheduling calls and more time for patients who are already in the clinic.

Would it be worth a quick look to see whether this could fit [Clinic Name]?

Best,

[Your Name]

Email 2 — Follow-up with a practical angle

Timing: 4 business days later

Subject line options:

  • Re: A simpler way to handle [Clinic Name]’s appointments
  • Less phone tag for your team
  • For your next busy clinic day

Body:

Hi [First Name],

Just following up in case scheduling is on your team’s list of small problems that quietly take up a lot of time.

The usual friction is simple: a patient calls while the front desk is busy, misses a reminder, or needs to move an appointment outside office hours.

[Tool] helps clinics make those routine changes easier for patients without adding another task for staff.

If improving appointment flow is a priority this quarter, would you be open to a 15-minute conversation?

Best,

[Your Name]

Email 3 — Close the loop

Timing: 6–7 business days later

Subject line options:

  • Should I close the loop?
  • Scheduling at [Clinic Name]
  • Worth revisiting later?

Body:

Hi [First Name],

I haven’t heard back, so I’ll assume improving scheduling isn’t a priority right now.

Before I close the loop: would it be useful to see how [Tool] could help [Clinic Name] manage online bookings, confirmations, and rescheduling?

If someone else handles scheduling decisions, I’d appreciate being pointed in the right direction.

Otherwise, no problem—I’ll leave you to it.

Best,

[Your Name]

Notes

  • Assumed the tool supports online booking, appointment confirmations, and rescheduling. Remove or adjust any feature that is not available.
  • Assumed the audience includes clinic owners, office managers, and practice managers.
  • Keep the sender’s signature simple and include one relevant link only, such as a booking page or product overview.
  • Personalize with the clinic name and, where possible, mention the clinic’s specialty or number of locations.
