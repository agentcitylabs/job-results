# One-Page Yoga Studio Booking Wireframe

**Goal:** Help visitors quickly find a suitable class, understand what to expect, and book a spot. Keep a **“Book a class”** button visible throughout the page, especially on mobile.

## 1. Header

- **Left:** Studio logo and name  
- **Right:** “Classes,” “Our studio,” and “Contact” links, plus a prominent **Book a class** button

**Why:** Gives visitors a quick sense of where they are and an obvious path to booking. On mobile, condense the links into a menu but keep the booking button easy to reach.

## 2. Intro and booking prompt

- **Headline:** “Find your flow at [Studio Name]”
- **Short description:** A warm, plain-language note about the studio and its classes
- **Primary button:** **See the schedule**
- **Optional secondary link:** “New to yoga? Start here”
- **Image:** A welcoming studio scene, ideally showing real people and the space

**Why:** Visitors should understand the studio’s feel and next step without scrolling. The schedule button can jump directly to the booking section.

## 3. Class schedule and booking

- **Heading:** “Choose a class”
- **Filters:** Day, class style, level, and instructor
- **Schedule list or cards:** Each class shows:
  - Class name and short description
  - Date and time
  - Instructor
  - Level or intensity
  - Duration
  - Price or pass requirement
  - Spots remaining, when relevant
  - **Book this class** button
- **Booking flow:** Selecting a class opens a simple booking form or checkout with the chosen class preselected

**Why:** This is the main purpose of the page, so it should appear early and make comparisons easy. Clear details help visitors choose confidently before they begin checkout.

## 4. New to the studio?

- A brief welcome message and what to bring
- Any beginner-friendly options or first-visit offer
- Link or button to **Book your first class**

**Why:** First-timers may hesitate if they don’t know what to expect. A little reassurance can turn uncertainty into a booking.

## 5. What to expect

- Three or four short points, such as:
  - Arrive 10 minutes early
  - Mats and props are available
  - Wear comfortable clothing
  - Tell the instructor about injuries or needs

**Why:** Answers common practical questions at a glance and helps visitors feel prepared.

## 6. Meet the instructors

- Small portraits, names, and one-line specialties or teaching styles

**Why:** Yoga is personal. A glimpse of the people leading class can help visitors find an instructor and feel more at ease.

## 7. Studio details

- Address and neighborhood
- Small map or map link
- Transit, parking, and accessibility notes
- Studio hours or arrival instructions

**Why:** Visitors need to know whether getting to class is convenient and what to expect when they arrive.

## 8. FAQs and booking policies

- Expandable answers for common questions: cancellations, late arrivals, payment, class levels, and missed classes
- Keep the cancellation policy clear and easy to spot

**Why:** Clear policies build trust and reduce surprises after someone books.

## 9. Closing booking prompt and footer

- **Closing line:** “Ready to roll out your mat?”
- **Button:** **Book a class**
- Footer with contact details, social links, and any required legal or accessibility links

**Why:** Gives visitors who have read through the page another direct route to book, while keeping essential studio information easy to find.
