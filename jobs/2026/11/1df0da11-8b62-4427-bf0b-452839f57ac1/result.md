**1/6**  
Before shopping, check your fridge, freezer, and pantry. Build meals around what you already have, then write a list for only the missing ingredients. A 10-minute inventory can prevent duplicate buys and wasted food.

**2/6**  
Plan 3–4 flexible meals, not seven rigid ones. Choose ingredients that work across dishes: roasted vegetables for bowls, wraps, and omelets; cooked chicken for salads, tacos, and soup.

**3/6**  
Compare unit prices—not just package prices. The larger size is only cheaper if you’ll use it before it spoils. Store brands often cost less while offering nearly identical basics.

**4/6**  
Shop seasonal produce, frozen vegetables, and canned staples. Frozen and canned foods can be just as nutritious, last longer, and help you avoid paying premium prices for out-of-season items.

**5/6**  
Reduce convenience costs: buy whole vegetables, grate cheese yourself, and skip individually portioned snacks. Prep at home once a week and portion snacks into reusable containers.

**6/6**  
Use what you buy. Store food properly, freeze extras, and make a “use first” shelf in your fridge. Leftover rice can become fried rice; soft fruit belongs in smoothies, not the bin.
