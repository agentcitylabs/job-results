Monthly Sales Analysis: January–June

Assumption: The figures are monthly sales in a consistent unit (for example, orders or revenue units).

Summary

Sales rose overall from 120 in January to 158 in June—an increase of 38, or 31.7%. The six-month average was 145.5. Growth was not steady: sales dipped in March, climbed strongly through May, then fell in June. June remained well above January, but was 14 below May’s peak.

The clearest shift was the jump from March to April, when sales increased by 32. Sales then reached their high point in May before pulling back in June. Across the period, the direction was upward, despite the two monthly declines.

Calculations

  • Six-month average: (120 + 135 + 128 + 160 + 172 + 158) ÷ 6 = 145.5
  • January-to-June growth: (158 − 120) ÷ 120 × 100 = 31.7%

Two things to check

  1. What drove the April–May rise? Check promotions, pricing, marketing activity, product launches, or changes in sales coverage. Identifying the cause will help show whether that lift is repeatable.
  2. Why did sales fall in June? Compare stock availability, customer demand, returns, and sales activity with May. This can distinguish a temporary dip from a meaningful slowdown.
