Summary

This launch plan targets casual mobile gamers who enjoy quick, low-pressure puzzle sessions.

It uses three channels: short-form video, app-store optimization, and creator/community outreach.

The first week focuses on awareness, installs, onboarding quality, and early retention.

Use the measurement framework below to decide what to scale, change, or stop after seven days.

Launch Objective

Generate qualified installs and learn whether new players reach the game’s core “fun moment” quickly enough to return.

Primary first-week targets:

  • Build initial awareness with a clear, recognizable gameplay hook.
  • Drive installs from audiences likely to enjoy short puzzle sessions.
  • Reach at least 35% Day 1 retention and 10–15% Day 7 retention as early benchmarks.
  • Identify the best-performing creative, audience, and store-page message before increasing paid spend.

Audience

Core audience

  • Adults aged roughly 18–44 who play mobile games in short sessions.
  • People who enjoy Wordle-style puzzles, match-and-clear mechanics, logic games, or relaxing brain teasers.
  • Players looking for a low-commitment activity during commutes, breaks, or before bed.
  • Casual players who value simple controls, satisfying progress, and frequent rewards over complex stories or competitive depth.

Key message

“A quick, satisfying puzzle break whenever you have a minute.”

Show the game in action immediately. Avoid describing features abstractly; demonstrate the puzzle, the decision the player makes, and the satisfying result.

Three Launch Channels

1. Short-form video

Use TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts to show 10–20-second gameplay clips.

Creative angles:

  • “Can you solve this in three moves?”
  • A visually satisfying chain, clear, or reveal.
  • A beginner puzzle followed by a surprisingly difficult one.
  • A quick before-and-after showing how the board changes.
  • A reaction or challenge format that invites viewers to comment with their solution.

Create at least five variations with different opening seconds, captions, and puzzle examples. End with a direct call to action: “Try today’s puzzle—download free.”

2. App-store optimization

Prepare the App Store and Google Play pages before sending traffic.

Priorities:

  • Put the core benefit in the first line of the description.
  • Make the first screenshot explain the game without requiring text-heavy reading.
  • Use screenshots to show the play loop: start puzzle → make a move → solve → earn progress.
  • Include keywords naturally around the game category and mechanic, such as “daily puzzle,” “brain game,” “logic puzzle,” or “relaxing puzzle,” where accurate.
  • Test two icon or screenshot treatments if the platform and traffic volume support it.

The store page should match the promise used in videos. If the ads show relaxing puzzles, the page should not lead with unrelated competitive features.

3. Creator and player communities

Contact small gaming creators, puzzle accounts, and relevant Reddit, Discord, or Facebook communities. Prioritize niche creators with active comments over large accounts with weak engagement.

Offer:

  • Early access or a creator code.
  • A simple challenge, such as solving a difficult level or beating a target score.
  • A shareable daily puzzle or screenshot.
  • Clear disclosure guidance for sponsored or incentivized posts.

Do not rely on one large creator. Test 10–20 small placements or outreach conversations and compare the quality of resulting players.

First-Week Action Plan

Day 1: Launch and baseline

  • Publish the game on the selected stores.
  • Confirm analytics events are firing for install, tutorial completion, first puzzle started, first puzzle completed, session start, purchase, ad view, and return visit.
  • Release the first short-form videos.
  • Monitor crashes, failed downloads, store-page errors, and tutorial drop-off hourly.
  • Respond to early reviews and support messages promptly.

Day 2: Review the first signals

  • Compare installs and store-page conversion by channel.
  • Identify where players leave the tutorial or first puzzle.
  • Check whether the first session reaches the game’s satisfying payoff within five minutes.
  • Pause creative that attracts views but produces weak store visits or installs.
  • Publish a second gameplay angle rather than repeating the launch post.

Day 3: Improve conversion

  • Refresh the weakest store screenshot or first line of copy.
  • Publish a challenge-based video using a real puzzle from the game.
  • Contact additional small creators and community moderators.
  • Fix any onboarding friction, confusing instructions, or early difficulty spikes.
  • Start collecting qualitative feedback from reviews and direct messages.

Day 4–5: Test and concentrate

  • Compare audiences and creative by cost per install, tutorial completion, and Day 1 retention.
  • Put more distribution behind the best-performing creative, not simply the one with the most views.
  • Run a limited event, such as a featured daily puzzle or seven-day streak.
  • Encourage players to share results without requiring personal data or spammy invites.
  • Release a small quality-of-life update only if it addresses a clear launch issue.

Day 6–7: Retention and decision point

  • Send one useful, non-intrusive reminder to players who started but did not return, if consent and platform rules allow.
  • Review Day 1 and early Day 7 retention by acquisition source.
  • Read every low-star review for repeated problems.
  • Decide which channel to scale, which message to revise, and which audience to stop targeting.
  • Produce a short launch report with actions for week two.

Measurement Framework

Track results by channel, campaign, creative, platform, and country where possible.

GoalMetricWhat it indicates
AwarenessVideo reach, three-second views, completion rateWhether the hook earns attention
Store interestStore-page visits and conversion to installWhether the message and listing align
AcquisitionCost per install and installs by sourceWhich channels efficiently create downloads
ActivationTutorial completion and first puzzle completionWhether players understand the game
EngagementSessions per user, puzzles completed, session lengthWhether the core loop is compelling
RetentionDay 1, Day 3, and Day 7 retentionWhether players have a reason to return
MonetizationAd views, purchases, and revenue per active userWhether the business model feels viable
QualityCrash rate, uninstall rate, rating, support issuesWhether technical or UX problems are blocking growth

Use retention and first-puzzle completion as the main quality signals. Cheap installs are not valuable if players quit before understanding the game.

Notes

  • Assumption: the game is free to download and available on both iOS and Android.
  • Assumption: it has a short tutorial, repeatable puzzles, and either ads, optional purchases, or both.
  • Set up privacy-compliant analytics and attribution before launch; platform reporting may be delayed or limited.
  • Benchmarks are directional for an early casual-game launch. Compare channels against one another and against the game’s own trend rather than treating them as guarantees.
  • Confirm that all creator promotions, reviews, incentives, and notifications follow platform rules and local advertising requirements.
