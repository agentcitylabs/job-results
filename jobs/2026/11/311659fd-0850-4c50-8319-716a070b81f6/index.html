10-Slide Pitch Deck Outline: Local Dog-Walking Marketplace

Assumption: The app launches in one city, matching dog owners with vetted local walkers for scheduled and on-demand walks.

1. The Problem: Finding Reliable Dog Care Is Hard

Key point: Dog owners struggle to find trustworthy, available, and convenient walkers—especially when work, travel, or emergencies disrupt their schedules.

  • Last-minute availability is difficult to find.
  • Trust is a major concern when handing over a pet and home access.
  • Existing options can be fragmented, expensive, or inconsistent.

2. The Solution: Trusted Walkers, Matched Locally

Key point: The app makes it simple to find, book, and pay a nearby dog walker who fits the owner’s needs.

  • Search by location, availability, price, experience, and dog requirements.
  • Review walker profiles and verified ratings.
  • Book recurring or one-time walks in a few taps.
  • Receive updates and walk completion notifications.

3. How It Works

Key point: A straightforward booking flow removes friction for both sides of the marketplace.

  1. Dog owner creates a dog profile.
  2. The app recommends suitable local walkers.
  3. Owner selects a time and confirms the booking.
  4. Walker completes the walk and shares an update.
  5. Payment and reviews are handled in-app.

4. Why Now

Key point: Changes in work patterns and rising pet ownership are increasing demand for flexible, dependable dog-care services.

  • More owners need help around hybrid and irregular work schedules.
  • Dogs remain a major household priority and expense.
  • Consumers are increasingly comfortable booking local services through mobile apps.
  • Independent walkers want flexible ways to earn income locally.

5. Target Customers

Key point: The initial customer is a busy dog owner who values convenience, trust, and reliable care.

Primary users

  • Professionals with demanding or hybrid schedules.
  • Apartment residents without easy outdoor access.
  • Frequent travelers and people with irregular hours.
  • Owners of high-energy, senior, or special-needs dogs.

Supply-side users

  • Experienced independent dog walkers.
  • Students and part-time workers.
  • Pet-care professionals seeking additional bookings.

6. Product and Trust Features

Key point: Safety and transparency are core to the product, not add-ons.

  • Identity and background verification for walkers.
  • Detailed dog and walker profiles.
  • In-app messaging and secure payments.
  • GPS-enabled walk tracking.
  • Photo and post-walk notes.
  • Ratings, reviews, incident reporting, and customer support.
  • Optional insurance or protection coverage.

7. Business Model

Key point: The company earns revenue from every completed booking while keeping pricing clear for owners and walkers.

Proposed model

  • Take a percentage commission from each booking.
  • Add optional premium features for dog owners, such as priority matching or discounted recurring walks.
  • Offer professional walkers enhanced visibility through paid tools or subscriptions.

Example transaction

  • Owner pays: $25 per walk
  • Walker receives: $20
  • Platform revenue: $5

Final pricing and commission should be tested by neighborhood and service type.

8. Go-to-Market Strategy

Key point: Build density neighborhood by neighborhood instead of launching too broadly with an empty marketplace.

Initial launch plan

  1. Recruit and verify a core group of walkers in one target area.
  2. Partner with apartment buildings, veterinarians, groomers, shelters, and pet retailers.
  3. Offer first-walk promotions and referral credits.
  4. Use local social media and community groups to attract dog owners.
  5. Focus on recurring bookings to improve retention and marketplace liquidity.

9. Competitive Advantage

Key point: Local matching, stronger trust signals, and a better owner experience can differentiate the app from generic gig platforms.

  • More relevant matches based on dog temperament, size, needs, and routine.
  • Faster access to genuinely nearby walkers.
  • Consistent profiles and quality standards.
  • Better communication before, during, and after each walk.
  • Data from completed bookings improves future matching and retention.

10. Vision and Funding Ask

Key point: Start with dog walking, then become the trusted local platform for everyday pet care.

Expansion opportunities

  • Pet sitting and boarding.
  • Puppy visits and training support.
  • Medication administration.
  • Group walks.
  • Grooming and veterinary transportation.

Closing message

The app can turn a stressful, trust-sensitive task into a reliable local service—while helping qualified walkers build flexible income.

Ask: Funding or support to launch in the first city, validate repeat usage, grow the walker network, and prove sustainable unit economics.

Pre-Presentation Checklist

  • [ ] Define the launch city and initial neighborhood.
  • [ ] Add evidence of dog-owner demand.
  • [ ] Include early user interviews, waitlist data, or pilot results.
  • [ ] Confirm proposed walk pricing and platform commission.
  • [ ] Explain walker screening and safety procedures.
  • [ ] Add a simple competitor comparison.
  • [ ] Include target metrics: bookings, repeat rate, acquisition cost, and contribution margin.
  • [ ] State the funding amount or specific support requested.
  • [ ] Use one clear product mockup or booking-flow visual.
  • [ ] End with a memorable, customer-focused value proposition.
