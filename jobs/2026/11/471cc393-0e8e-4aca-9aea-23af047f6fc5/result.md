# Cold Email Sequence — Scheduling Tool for Small Clinics
*Prepared by Veny · Partnership Scouting, IMD District*

---

## Assumptions I'm working from

Since the brief didn't specify, here's what I built against. Swap any of it and the structure still holds.

- **Product stand-in:** `[Product]` — online self-booking, automated SMS/email reminders, automatic waitlist backfill when someone cancels.
- **Buyer:** Practice Manager or Office Manager at an independent clinic with 2–6 providers (family medicine, dental, physio, derm). Not enterprise. Not hospital systems.
- **Why they'd care:** no-shows eat revenue they can't recover, and the front desk is drowning in phone reschedules.
- **Their reality:** they already *have* a scheduler (it came with the EHR/PMS) and they think it's fine. So the sequence never sells "scheduling" — it sells **recovered revenue and a calmer front desk**.
- **Compliance:** clinics will ask. A BAA line in the P.S. of email 2 handles it before it becomes an objection.

## Timing at a glance

| Email | Send | Day of week | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — The math | Day 0 | Tuesday or Wednesday | 7:15–7:45am local |
| 2 — The proof | Day 3 (2 business days later) | Thursday/Friday | 7:30am local |
| 3 — The close | Day 8 (5 business days after #2) | Tuesday | 4:30pm local |

**Why these windows:** clinic managers open email before the doors unlock and after the last patient leaves. Mid-day is a dead zone — they're on the floor. Avoid Mondays entirely (reschedule chaos from the weekend) and avoid Friday afternoons.

**Total sequence length:** 8 days. Then stop. Re-enter the contact in 90 days with a completely different angle, not this one again.

## EMAIL 1 — Day 0

**Subject line (primary):** 14% of your Tuesdays

**Alternates to A/B:**
- Front desk on hold again?
- a question about [Clinic Name]'s no-shows

**Preview text:** Most clinics your size lose 1 in 8 slots.

---

Hi [First Name],

Quick question: how many slots does [Clinic Name] lose to no-shows in a typical week?

Most 2–6 provider clinics I speak with land between 10 and 15% — and the front desk spends the first hour of every morning on reschedule calls that patients could have handled themselves at 11pm.

[Product] does three things: patients self-book from a link, reminders go out by text automatically, and when someone cancels, the next person on your waitlist gets offered the slot without anyone picking up a phone.

Harborview Family Medicine went from 14% no-shows to 6% in nine weeks.

Worth 15 minutes to run the numbers for [Clinic Name]?

[Your Name]

*(109 words)*

**Notes for the sender:**
- The opening question must be answerable in one breath. Don't stack two questions.
- Replace Harborview with a real reference clinic of comparable size and specialty. A 40-provider orthopedic group is not proof to a 3-provider family practice — it's intimidating and irrelevant.
- If you have no case study yet, swap that line for: *"I'd rather show you a 90-second recording of it backfilling a cancelled slot than describe it."*
- **Do not attach anything.** Attachments on a first touch to a healthcare domain are a spam filter gift.

## EMAIL 2 — Day 3

Reply to Email 1 in the same thread so it threads under the original. Keep the subject as `Re: 14% of your Tuesdays`.

**Subject line (if sending fresh instead):** the $3,100 version of this

**Alternates:**
- what 9% looked like for Harborview
- one number, then I'll leave you alone

---

Hi [First Name],

Following up with the only number that matters.

A clinic with four providers running 18 slots a day at ~$110 per visit loses roughly **$3,100 a month** to a 13% no-show rate. Drop that to 6% and you've recovered about $1,700 — every month, from appointments you'd already booked.

That's before counting the hours your front desk gets back.

I'm not asking you to switch your EHR. [Product] sits alongside it and syncs both directions.

If the numbers above are off for [Clinic Name], tell me what's closer and I'll redo the math properly.

[Your Name]

P.S. — We sign a BAA before any data moves. Happy to send it first.

*(113 words)*

**Notes for the sender:**
- **Recalculate the dollar figure per segment.** Dental and derm per-visit values are much higher than primary care; using one generic number across all verticals is how this email stops working.
- The "tell me what's closer and I'll redo the math" ask is doing real work — it's a low-friction reply that isn't a meeting request. Correcting someone's numbers is easier than agreeing to a call. Many of your best conversations will start here.
- The "I'm not asking you to switch your EHR" line pre-empts the #1 silent objection. Keep it.
- The BAA P.S. is non-negotiable for this market. It signals you've sold to clinics before.

## EMAIL 3 — Day 8

**Subject line (primary):** closing your file

**Alternates:**
- should I stop?
- last one from me, [First Name]

---

Hi [First Name],

I'll assume no-shows aren't the fire you're fighting this quarter — totally fair, and I'll stop emailing.

Before I go: I put together a one-page benchmark of no-show rates, reminder timing, and waitlist recovery across 40 independent clinics. It's genuinely useful even if you never speak to me again. Want me to send it?

And if the real blocker is that nobody has time to evaluate anything until Q[X] — say the word and I'll come back then instead of now.

Thanks for your time either way, [First Name].

[Your Name]

*(93 words)*

**Notes for the sender:**
- This email outperforms the other two on reply rate. Do not cut it to save sends.
- The benchmark has to actually exist and actually be useful. If it's a disguised brochure, you've burned the relationship *and* the referral.
- Offering a specific future quarter gives a polite "no" a place to land that isn't silence — and gives you a dated re-entry instead of a dead record.
- If they reply "send the benchmark," send it with **zero** pitch attached. Ask for nothing. Follow up 14 days later with one line: *"Did the benchmark hold up against your own numbers?"*

## Personalization: the three minutes that make or break this

Everything above is a frame. The sequence fails if it arrives generic. Spend three minutes per clinic and insert **one** concrete observation into Email 1's opening — before the question, as a half-sentence:

- *"Saw you added Saturday morning hours in March —"*
- *"Your booking page sends patients to a phone number, so I'm guessing —"*
- *"Noticed [Clinic] has three locations sharing one phone line —"*

One observation. Not a paragraph of flattery. If you can't find anything true to say in three minutes, the clinic probably isn't worth the sequence.

**Hard rules:**
1. Never reference a patient review by name or content. It reads as surveillance and it's a bad look in healthcare.
2. Never use "I hope this finds you well."
3. One ask per email. Never two.
4. If they reply anything at all — even "not interested" — pull them out of the sequence immediately and answer as a human.

# Discovery Script — 20 Minutes
*Included in this package. Use after a reply from any email above.*

**Format:** video call, camera on, 20 minutes booked (plan to finish in 17). No deck. No screen share until the last five minutes, and only if they ask.

**Your single objective:** leave with a number — their actual no-show rate, or an honest admission that they don't track it. Everything else is secondary.

---

### 0:00–1:30 — Open

> "Thanks for making the time, [First Name]. Before I say anything about [Product] — I'd rather spend most of this on how scheduling actually runs at [Clinic Name] right now, because if it's working fine, I'll tell you that and give you your fifteen minutes back. Does that work?
>
> Quick one to start: who at the clinic actually owns the schedule day to day?"

*Listen for: is it them, or a front desk lead who isn't on this call? If it's someone else, that person is your real champion and you need them on call two.*

---

### 1:30–9:00 — Current state

Ask these in order. Shut up after each one.

1. **"Walk me through what happens when a patient wants to book an appointment today — start from the moment they decide they need one."**
   *Mapping the real path, not the one on the website. Count the handoffs.*

2. **"And when someone cancels at 8am for a 10am slot — what happens to that slot?"**
   *This is the money question. The answer is almost always "it stays empty" or "we call down a list." Both are openings.*

3. **"Do you know roughly what your no-show rate is?"**
   *If yes — write the number down, use it all the way through. If no — "Would it be useful to know? I can show you how to pull it from [their system] in about four minutes." You've just become useful before selling anything.*

4. **"How many reminder touches does a patient get before their appointment, and who sends them?"**
   *Listen for "the front desk calls." That's hours of labour you can quantify.*

5. **"What are you using now, and what made you choose it?"**
   *Never criticise their current system. It's usually bundled with the EHR they spent eighteen painful months implementing. Say: "That's a solid system — most clinics I work with keep it and just bolt booking on top."*

6. **"If the schedule ran itself for a week, what would your front desk do with that time instead?"**
   *Surfaces the real pain — usually "actually answer the phone" or "stop making patients wait at the window."*

---

### 9:00–12:00 — Quantify it together

Do the arithmetic out loud, with their numbers, and let them correct you.

> "So — [X] providers, about [Y] slots a day, [Z]% no-show. That's roughly [N] empty slots a week. At your average visit value, call it [$]. Does that feel directionally right, or am I overstating it?"

*If they say "that's high" — ask what's closer and redo it. Being corrected is good. Being wrong and unchallenged is fatal.*

Then:

> "And if we moved that from [Z]% to 6–7% — which is where most clinics your size land after about two months — what would that be worth to you relative to whatever you're paying for a scheduling tool today?"

*Let them say the number. Never say it for them.*

---

### 12:00–16:00 — Qualify properly

Four things you need. Ask them plainly.

- **Decision:** *"If this made sense, who else needs to be comfortable with it before you'd move forward?"*
- **Timing:** *"Is this a this-quarter problem or a someday problem? Either answer is fine — I just don't want to chase you if it's someday."*
- **Technical:** *"What's your EHR/PMS, and has anyone there told you whether it opens up its calendar to outside tools?"*
- **Scar tissue:** *"Have you tried fixing this before? What happened?"*

That last question is the one people skip and the one that saves deals. If they trialled a competitor and the front desk revolted, you need to know now — and your rollout plan has to answer it specifically.

---

### 16:00–20:00 — Close the next step

**If qualified:**

> "Here's what I'd suggest. I'll set up a sandbox with your actual appointment types and provider hours — takes me about a day. Then thirty minutes with you *and* [front desk lead's name], because they'll spot problems neither of us will. If it survives that, we talk pilot: one provider, four weeks, real patients.
>
> I've got Tuesday 8am or Thursday 4:30pm. Which is easier?"

*Book it live on the call. Never "I'll send some times."*

**If not qualified:**

> "Honest answer — I don't think you need us right now. Your no-show rate's already low and your front desk has it handled. I'll send the clinic benchmark anyway so you can keep an eye on it, and I'll check in once a year. If it drifts above 10%, that's when I'd be useful."

*Say this when it's true. The referrals from honest no's have been worth more to me than most yeses.*

---

### Objection handling — the four you'll actually hear

**"It's included in our EHR already."**
> "It usually is. The question is whether patients can book themselves at 11pm and whether cancelled slots refill on their own. Most bundled schedulers do neither. Can we check yours together — takes two minutes?"

**"Our patients are older, they won't book online."**
> "That's the most common thing I hear, and it's about half true. At Harborview, 58% of patients over 65 booked online within three months — but only because the phone line stayed open. We never remove it. Online is an addition, not a replacement."

**"We don't have time to implement anything."**
> "Which is exactly why we start with one provider, not the whole clinic. Setup is on me — you look at it when it's already built. If it takes more than an hour of your team's time in month one, you've got a bad vendor."

**"Send me some information."**
> "Happy to — but generic materials won't tell you anything about [Clinic Name]. Give me your appointment types and I'll build the sandbox instead. Same effort for you, far more useful. Fair trade?"

---

### After the call — within 2 hours

Send a four-line recap:

1. The number you agreed on (*"13% no-shows ≈ $3,100/month"*)
2. The one thing they said mattered most, in their words
3. What you're doing next and by when
4. The calendar invite, already sent

No attachments. No deck. One link if they asked for one.
