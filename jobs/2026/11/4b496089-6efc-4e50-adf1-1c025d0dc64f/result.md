# Casual Puzzle Game: One-Page Launch Plan

**Assumption:** A free-to-play mobile puzzle game launching on iOS and Android, with short levels, a modest marketing budget, and no existing audience.

**Launch goal:** Find the puzzle hook that attracts players who return—not just people who download. Use week one to identify a repeatable creative format and spot friction in the first session.

## Audience

**Primary:** Adults aged 25–44 who enjoy approachable logic, sorting, or matching games during commutes, lunch breaks, and evening downtime.

**What they want:** A satisfying challenge in a few minutes, clear rules, and a sense of progress without a long tutorial.

**Positioning:** “A little brain workout. A satisfying little win.”

Show the actual game immediately. Lead with an interesting decision, then reveal the satisfying result. Avoid fake levels or difficulty claims the game cannot deliver.

## Three Launch Channels

| Channel | Role and launch content | How to measure |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** | Discovery and creative testing. Post one 10–20-second gameplay video daily. Test three hooks: “Which move would you make?”, “One move fixes this,” and a clean, satisfying solve. Put the puzzle on screen in the first second. | Opening hold rate where available, average watch time, completion rate, shares, profile visits, and tracked store clicks. Compare posts of similar length. |
| **Instagram Reels** | Reach casual players and make the game easy to share. Publish four native versions of the strongest gameplay clips, without watermarks. Use large captions and a simple prompt: “Send this to your puzzle person.” | Reach, shares per 1,000 views, saves, profile visits, and tracked store clicks. Prioritize shares and clicks over likes. |
| **Apple App Store + Google Play** | Turn curiosity into installs. Use a clear icon and three opening screenshots that show the core puzzle, a satisfying outcome, and progression. Match the promise in the videos to the store page. | Product-page-to-install conversion by platform, attributed installs where available, first-session completion, and day-one retention. |

**Creative rule:** Change one element at a time—opening line, puzzle, or reveal—so a stronger result teaches us something.

## First-Week Actions

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| **1 — Launch cleanly** | Verify both store listings and download links. Test analytics on real devices. Publish the first “Which move?” video on TikTok and Reels. Check crashes, loading, and tutorial completion before pushing more traffic. |
| **2 — Test satisfaction** | Publish a satisfying-solve TikTok. Reply to comments with useful hints, not repeated download pitches. Review where new players leave the opening session. |
| **3 — Test challenge** | Publish the “One move fixes this” hook on both platforms. Review early viewing and click data. If players consistently stall at the same tutorial step, prioritize a fix. |
| **4 — Repeat the strongest format** | Make a new TikTok using the best-performing opening, with a different puzzle. Judge it on watch quality **and** store interest—not raw views alone. |
| **5 — Turn comments into content** | Answer a recurring player question with a gameplay video on both platforms. Review store conversion separately for iOS and Android; flag weak screenshots or a mismatch between content and gameplay. |
| **6 — Make it repeatable** | Publish the first “Daily Puzzle Break” TikTok. Invite viewers to choose their move before the reveal. Prepare three follow-up episodes using real levels. |
| **7 — Review and choose** | Post the strongest format on both platforms with a fresh level. Summarize results, select two formats for week two, and list the top three onboarding or product fixes. |

## Measurement and Week-Two Decisions

**Before launch:** Track `first_open`, `tutorial_complete`, `level_start`, and `level_complete`, alongside session and crash data. Use distinct campaign links for TikTok and Instagram. Report unattributed installs separately; mobile attribution will not capture every journey.

**Daily scorecard:**
- **Acquisition:** Views → tracked store clicks → installs.
- **Activation:** Tutorial completions ÷ first opens.
- **Retention:** Players returning the calendar day after first open ÷ players in that install cohort.
- **Quality:** Crash-free sessions and early-level drop-off.

**Provisional checkpoints—not forecasts:** Aim for at least **75% tutorial completion** and **25% day-one retention**. Treat small cohorts as directional; show player counts beside percentages. Evaluate day-one retention only for cohorts old enough to have returned.

**Decision rules:**
- **Strong views, few clicks:** Make the gameplay benefit and download prompt clearer.
- **Clicks, few installs:** Improve store-page clarity and message match.
- **Installs, weak activation or retention:** Fix the opening experience before increasing promotion.
- **Healthy retention plus repeatable creative results:** Continue the two winning formats and consider a small paid test.

**Week-one success:** A working measurement baseline, two promising creative formats, and a clear answer to what should improve before the next push.
