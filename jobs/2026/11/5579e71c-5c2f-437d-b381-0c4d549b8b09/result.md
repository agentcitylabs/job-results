# Discovery Call Script: Restaurant Website Maintenance

*Assumption: You’re speaking with the owner or manager of an independent restaurant. The goal is to understand their needs and agree on a next step—not sell a package before you know what needs attention.*

## Opening

“Hi [Name], it’s [Your Name] from [Business]. We help small restaurants keep their websites working and their menus, hours and booking links up to date—without the owner having to handle every change.

“Have you got five minutes now? I’d like to understand how you manage the site and see whether we could take anything off your plate.”

**If they’re busy:**

“No problem—I don’t want to catch you during service. Would [specific time] or [specific time] be better for a quick call?”

## Five Discovery Questions

Ask these conversationally, leaving room for the owner to answer.

1. **“When your menu, prices or opening hours change, who updates the website—and how quickly does that usually happen?”**  
   Listen for delays, reliance on a former employee, or the owner doing it after hours.

2. **“What do you most want people to do on your site: book a table, place an order, check the menu or call you?”**  
   Identify the customer action that matters most. Use their answer when describing the value of maintenance.

3. **“Have you had any recent website headaches, like an old menu showing up, a booking link failing or pages being awkward on a phone?”**  
   If they mention a problem, acknowledge the specific inconvenience rather than jumping straight into a pitch.

4. **“Who currently looks after the behind-the-scenes work—updates, backups and checking that the site still works?”**  
   Don’t assume nobody does. There may already be a provider, but unclear responsibilities.

5. **“If someone reliable took over the upkeep, what would you want included—and what monthly spend would feel reasonable?”**  
   If they’re unsure about budget, don’t press. Note their priorities and explain that you’ll put a clear scope and price in writing.

## Bridge to the Offer

“Let me check I’ve understood. You want [main customer action] to work smoothly, but [specific problem] is making that harder. And you’d rather not spend your evenings [task they mentioned].

“Based on that, I’d suggest maintenance focused on [two or three relevant tasks—for example, menu updates, backups and booking-link checks]. We’d agree exactly what’s included and how quickly changes get handled, so you know what you’re paying for.”

*Only mention services you actually provide. Keep redesigns, new features and third-party booking-system support separate unless explicitly included.*

## Close the Call

### If there’s a clear fit

“The next step would be for me to review the public-facing site and send you a short proposal: what I’d maintain, the monthly price and anything outside the package. No login details needed at this stage.

“Shall I send that to [email] by [day], then give you a quick call on [day/time] to talk it through?”

### If they want to think about it

“Of course. I’ll send a short recap focused on [their main concern], with the scope and price clearly laid out. Would you prefer one follow-up next week, or to come back to me when you’re ready?”

### If they already have everything covered

“It sounds like you’ve got a setup that’s working. I wouldn’t suggest changing it just for the sake of it. Thanks for taking a few minutes—and if keeping the site current starts taking too much time, you’re welcome to get in touch.”

**Finish by confirming the agreed action:**

“Thanks, [Name]. I’ll [agreed action] by [date]. Enjoy the rest of your day.”
