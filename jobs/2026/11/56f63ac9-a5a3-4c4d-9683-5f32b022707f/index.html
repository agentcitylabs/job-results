The work, as delivered

The deliverable contains two ad versions, each with a headline, body copy, call to action and test hypothesis. It uses GroveMeal as a replaceable brand name and explicitly states assumptions about the subscription, ingredients, cooking time and audience. It also proposes metrics, a test duration and a follow-up approach if the versions tie.

  • Version A focuses on saving time and reducing dinner preparation effort.
  • Version A pairs a 20-minute dinner headline with “Claim your first box.”
  • Version B focuses on taste and trust, using sensory descriptions to challenge expectations of bland healthy food.
  • Version B uses the call to action “Taste this week’s menu.”
  • The testing plan proposes at least 7 days or 1,000 impressions per version, with the same audience and placement.
  • The suggested winner criteria are higher click-through rate and lower cost per first box; a tie would lead to testing A’s headline with B’s body.

A/B Ad Copy — Healthy Meal-Kit Service

Assumption: The offer is a weekly subscription of pre-portioned, mostly organic ingredients for 2–4 people. Meals cook in 20–25 minutes with one pan or sheet. Target is busy adults 28–45 who want to eat better without another grocery trip or another night of takeout. Brand name used in the ads: GroveMeal. (Swap the name if yours is different; the rest of the copy stands.)

Version A

Headline: Dinner’s already measured. You’re just 20 minutes from the table.

Body: Organic vegetables, real proteins, and a recipe card that doesn’t assume you have a Saturday free. Everything arrives portioned. You cook, you eat, you don’t spend Thursday night staring into the fridge.

CTA: Claim your first box

What A tests: Time-and-friction angle. The hypothesis is that the dominant objection is “I don’t have the hours,” not “I don’t care about health.” Concrete minutes (20) plus the image of the 6 p.m. fridge stare should outperform a more abstract “eat healthy” promise with this audience. Watch CTR and add-to-cart on mobile, especially 5–8 p.m.

Version B

Headline: Food that still tastes like it came from a kitchen, not a warehouse.

Body: Named vegetables, herbs you can smell when you open the bag, sauces you actually want to scrape from the pan. No mystery packets. No “healthy” that means cardboard. Just plates you’d be happy to serve someone you like.

CTA: Taste this week’s menu

What B tests: Taste-and-trust angle versus the “sad diet food” stereotype. The hypothesis is that health-conscious buyers have been burned by bland kits and will click when the copy names sensory details (herbs, scrape-the-pan, named vegetables) instead of macros or minutes. Watch CTR from women 30–42 and conversion on the menu-preview page; if they linger on photos, B is working.

Run both for at least 7 days or 1,000 impressions each, same audience and placement. Winner is the version with higher CTR and lower cost-per-first-box, not vanity likes. If they tie, keep the body from B and the headline from A for a third round.
