The work, as delivered

The delivery contains two 15-second ad scripts, each laid out in seven shots with timings, visuals, voiceover, on-screen titles and motion notes. The first uses a confidence-focused hook for beginners and cold audiences; the second uses a conversation-focused hook for travelers, false beginners and warm audiences. It also includes explanations of each hook and notes on generic branding, example languages and approximate timing.

  • Version 1 opens with “You already know 1,000 words” and uses Spanish examples.
  • Version 2 opens with “Stop saying ‘I don’t speak it’” and follows a French market conversation.
  • Each script includes an audience recommendation.
  • Both scripts end with an app-logo card and a “Start free” call to action.
  • Shot tables specify title animations alongside visuals and audio.
  • Notes explain how to replace the generic branding and example languages.

Summary

Two 15-second ad scripts for your language-learning app, each with a different opening hook and a clearly matched audience. Version 1 (“You already know 1,000 words”) is a confidence-first script for beginners and cold audiences. Version 2 (“Stop saying ‘I don’t speak it’”) is a conversation-first script for travelers and false beginners who freeze in real situations. Each script is 7 shots, includes motion-title notes, and can be cut from your 90-second master or shot as a standalone.

Version 1 — The “You Already Know It” Hook

Opening hook: “You already know 1,000 words. You just don’t know you know them.”

Best for: Complete beginners, people who think they’re “bad at languages,” travelers with an upcoming trip, and cold audiences who need a quick reason to believe the app is for them.

Version 2 — The “Stop Killing Conversations” Hook

Opening hook: “Stop saying ‘I don’t speak it.’ That’s the sentence that ends every conversation.”

Best for: Travelers who have some basics but freeze in real moments, “false beginners” who know vocabulary but can’t speak, and warm audiences who already know the app and need a specific reason to re-engage.

Why this hook works: It names a specific, embarrassing moment that every traveler has experienced — the moment the conversation dies. The viewer thinks, That’s me. Then the script shows a tiny, concrete fix: one phrase, three words, and the door stays open.

Notes

  • Assumed a generic app — no brand name was given, so all scripts say “the app.” Swap in the product name and logo on the end card.
  • Assumed example languages — Version 1 uses Spanish, Version 2 uses French. If your app’s main language is different, replace the example phrases and keep the same structure.
  • Shot counts are intentionally low — each script is 7 shots, well under the 15-shot maximum. This leaves room for the editor to add cutaways, a logo sting, or localized variants without breaking the 15-second runtime.
  • Timings are approximate — the moments listed are for pacing, not frame-accurate editing. The title motions are described lightly so the animator can interpret them in the app’s existing style.
  • These can open the 90-second version — if you’re producing the longer video, each 15-second script works as a cold open. From there, expand with more phrase examples, a user story, or a second character.
