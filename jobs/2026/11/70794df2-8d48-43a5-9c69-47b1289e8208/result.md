## Summary

Two 15-second ad scripts for your language-learning app, each with a different opening hook and a clearly matched audience. **Version 1** (“You already know 1,000 words”) is a confidence-first script for beginners and cold audiences. **Version 2** (“Stop saying ‘I don’t speak it’”) is a conversation-first script for travelers and false beginners who freeze in real situations. Each script is 7 shots, includes motion-title notes, and can be cut from your 90-second master or shot as a standalone.

---

## Version 1 — The “You Already Know It” Hook

**Opening hook:** “You already know 1,000 words. You just don’t know you know them.”

**Best for:** Complete beginners, people who think they’re “bad at languages,” travelers with an upcoming trip, and cold audiences who need a quick reason to believe the app is for them.

| Shot | Time | Visual | Audio / VO | On-screen title & motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3s | Close-up of a Spanish menu. Hands hold it slightly too tight. Camera shakes a little, like the viewer is nervous. | VO: “You don’t know Spanish.” SFX: low, tense drone. | Title slams in: **“OR DO YOU?”** — fast upward wipe with a small overshoot, like a gasp. |
| 2 | 3–5s | Quick split screen: a restaurant sign on the left, headphones on the right. | VO: “Restaurante. Música. Hotel.” | Words pop in over each image: **“restaurante”**, **“música”**, **“hotel”** — each one landing on the beat, then shrinking to show its English twin underneath. |
| 3 | 5–7s | Cut to a person at a café table, face changing from worried to surprised. | VO: “You already know hundreds of Spanish words.” | Small animated counter appears in the corner: **“1,000+ words”** counting up quickly. |
| 4 | 7–9s | Over-the-shoulder shot of a phone. The app’s camera is scanning a street sign. | VO: “The app finds the words you already know.” | A highlighted box appears around the sign, and a label draws itself: **“YOU KNOW 47 WORDS HERE.”** The number counts up from 0 to 47 in the app’s accent color. |
| 5 | 9–11s | App screen: a lesson card that says “Café con leche” with a small audio waveform. | VO: “Then it starts your first lesson with a word you already know.” | The card flips in gently. The words **“CAFÉ CON LECHE”** sit still while the waveform pulses under them. |
| 6 | 11–13s | Real-life shot: the same person walks up to a counter and orders coffee. The barista smiles. | VO: “Day one — you order coffee.” | Title slides in from the left: **“DAY ONE.”** A small coffee cup icon clinks into place next to it. |
| 7 | 13–15s | End card: app logo centered on a warm, bright background. | VO: “Start free. You’re closer than you think.” SFX: soft, confident click. | Logo drops in with a slight bounce. Below it, title pops word by word: **“YOU’RE CLOSER THAN YOU THINK.”** Each word lands left to right with a tiny spring. |

**Why this hook works:** It flips the fear of “I don’t know anything” into a discovery. The viewer’s first reaction is, *Wait, I do know that.* That feeling of recognition is the entire ad.

## Version 2 — The “Stop Killing Conversations” Hook

**Opening hook:** “Stop saying ‘I don’t speak it.’ That’s the sentence that ends every conversation.”

**Best for:** Travelers who have some basics but freeze in real moments, “false beginners” who know vocabulary but can’t speak, and warm audiences who already know the app and need a specific reason to re-engage.

| Shot | Time | Visual | Audio / VO | On-screen title & motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3s | A traveler at a market stall. They say, in English, “Sorry, I don’t speak French.” The vendor’s face closes off. | VO: “This is the sentence that ends every conversation.” | Title slams in with a red slash through it: **“DON’T SAY THIS.”** The slash draws itself quickly, like a strike-through. |
| 2 | 3–5s | Same traveler, now looking at the app. A phrase appears: “Je voudrais ça.” | VO: “In the app, you learn one phrase that keeps the door open.” | The phrase types itself out: **“Je voudrais ça.”** A small speaker icon pulses next to it. |
| 3 | 5–7s | The traveler points at a woven bag at the stall and says, “Je voudrais ça.” The vendor’s face softens into a smile. | VO: “One phrase. You’re in the conversation.” | Title rises from the bottom: **“ONE PHRASE.”** It locks in with a soft hit, and a checkmark draws itself at the end. |
| 4 | 7–9s | App screen showing the phrase broken into parts: “Je voudrais” = “I would like” / “ça” = “that.” | VO: “Three words, and each one is taught in context.” | The phrase splits apart. **“Je voudrais”** slides left, **“ça”** slides right, and their English meanings fade in underneath. |
| 5 | 9–11s | Close-up of the traveler’s ear and then the vendor replying in French. The traveler doesn’t panic. | VO: “The app trains your ear until the reply feels natural.” | A sound wave appears between the two people. Title fades in at the bottom: **“TRAINED TO LISTEN.”** |
| 6 | 11–13s | The traveler laughs, nods, and says, “Oui!” The vendor hands over the bag. | VO: “And suddenly, you’re talking.” | Title bounces in with a spring: **“YOU’RE TALKING.”** The word “TALKING” is in a brighter color, and it stretches slightly when it lands. |
| 7 | 13–15s | End card: app logo with the tagline. | VO: “Learn the phrases that start conversations. Start free.” SFX: warm, open chime. | Logo appears with a smooth fade-up. Below it, title types on: **“START FREE.”** A small arrow slides out from under the button. |

**Why this hook works:** It names a specific, embarrassing moment that every traveler has experienced — the moment the conversation dies. The viewer thinks, *That’s me.* Then the script shows a tiny, concrete fix: one phrase, three words, and the door stays open.

## Notes

- **Assumed a generic app** — no brand name was given, so all scripts say “the app.” Swap in the product name and logo on the end card.
- **Assumed example languages** — Version 1 uses Spanish, Version 2 uses French. If your app’s main language is different, replace the example phrases and keep the same structure.
- **Shot counts are intentionally low** — each script is 7 shots, well under the 15-shot maximum. This leaves room for the editor to add cutaways, a logo sting, or localized variants without breaking the 15-second runtime.
- **Timings are approximate** — the moments listed are for pacing, not frame-accurate editing. The title motions are described lightly so the animator can interpret them in the app’s existing style.
- **These can open the 90-second version** — if you’re producing the longer video, each 15-second script works as a cold open. From there, expand with more phrase examples, a user story, or a second character.
