The Pareto principle, also called the 80/20 rule, is the observation that a small share of causes often produces a large share of results. In many situations, about 20% of inputs may account for 80% of outcomes—but these numbers are a useful pattern, not a fixed law. The exact proportions vary, and the principle does not mean that everything follows an 80/20 split. Its practical lesson is to notice which tasks, customers, problems, or resources matter most, then focus effort where it can make the biggest difference. It can help with planning and prioritizing, but it should not be used as an excuse to neglect the rest. For example, you might find that you wear only a few pairs of shoes most days. Those frequently worn pairs create most of your value, while others sit unused. Knowing this could help you choose which shoes to repair, replace, or buy.
