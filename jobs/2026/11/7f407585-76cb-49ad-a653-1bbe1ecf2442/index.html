The work, as delivered

The delivered script includes an opening, five discovery questions and a closing for a website maintenance sales call with a small restaurant. Each question includes listening prompts and a follow-up question. The closing proposes a website review and a priced recommendation, with separate responses for prospects who are ready to proceed or need more time.

  • Opening introduces website upkeep and asks permission to explore the restaurant’s needs.
  • Questions cover current responsibilities, recent problems, customer actions, staff time and desired support.
  • Listening prompts address issues such as delayed updates, broken booking links and mobile usability.
  • Follow-ups explore the impact of website problems and expectations for support.
  • Closing summarises priorities and proposes a website review before confirming scope and monthly price.
  • Alternative endings cover proceeding with setup or sending a summary for consideration.

Discovery Call Script: Website Maintenance for Small Restaurants

Opening

You:

“Hi, [Name], thanks for taking the time to speak with me. I work with small restaurants to keep their websites accurate, fast, and easy for customers to use—so your team doesn’t have to worry about outdated menus, broken booking links, or missed enquiries.

I’d like to learn how you currently manage the site, where things become difficult, and whether ongoing maintenance would be useful. I’ll ask a few quick questions, then I can suggest the simplest next step. Does that sound okay?”

Five Discovery Questions

1. Current website responsibilities

You:

“Who currently looks after the website, and how do you handle updates when something changes—such as the menu, opening hours, prices, or booking information?”

Listen for:

  • The owner handles everything personally
  • Updates are delayed or forgotten
  • Staff members are unsure how to make changes
  • The restaurant relies on a designer for every small edit

Follow-up:

“How long does a typical update take from noticing the change to having it live?”

2. Recent problems

You:

“Have you had any recent website issues, such as a broken booking form, incorrect opening hours, slow loading, or customers struggling to find information?”

Listen for:

  • Lost reservations or enquiries
  • Customers calling to confirm basic information
  • Problems after plugin or platform updates
  • A website that works poorly on mobile

Follow-up:

“What impact did that have on the team or on customers?”

3. Customer journey

You:

“What do you most want customers to do when they visit your website—book a table, order takeaway, view the menu, call you, or find directions?”

Listen for:

  • A primary business goal
  • Several competing calls to action
  • Important links that are difficult to find
  • A mismatch between the website and the restaurant’s priorities

Follow-up:

“Do you feel the website makes that action straightforward on a phone?”

4. Time and priorities

You:

“How much time does website upkeep currently take you or your staff each month?”

Listen for:

  • Regular interruptions
  • Updates being postponed during busy periods
  • No one being responsible for backups, security, or monitoring
  • Frustration with technical tasks

Follow-up:

“If those tasks were handled for you, what would your team spend that time on instead?”

5. Ideal support

You:

“If you had reliable website support, what would you want included—for example, routine content updates, security and backups, performance checks, or quick help when something breaks?”

Listen for:

  • Must-have services
  • Response-time expectations
  • Seasonal needs, such as menu changes or holiday hours
  • Concerns about cost or being tied into a complicated service

Follow-up:

“Is there anything about your current website or previous web support that you definitely would not want repeated?”

Closing the Call

You:

“Thanks, that gives me a clear picture. From what you’ve told me, the main priorities are [summarise two or three needs—for example: keeping the menu and opening hours current, protecting the site with backups, and having someone respond quickly when an issue appears].”

“Based on that, I’d recommend a straightforward maintenance plan covering [specific services]. We would take care of the routine technical work and agreed content updates, so your team can focus on running the restaurant.”

“The next step would be for me to review the current website, confirm exactly what needs attention, and send you a simple recommendation with the monthly price. If it looks right, we can schedule the initial cleanup and maintenance setup. Would you be happy for me to prepare that?”

If They Are Ready to Proceed

“Excellent. I’ll confirm the agreed services, access details, and start date in writing. We’ll begin with a review of the site, backups, security, and any urgent updates, then keep you informed as we go.”

If They Need Time

“Of course. I’ll send a short summary of what we discussed, including the recommended scope and price. The key benefit is that your website stays accurate and dependable without pulling you or your staff away from the restaurant. Feel free to come back to me with any questions.”
