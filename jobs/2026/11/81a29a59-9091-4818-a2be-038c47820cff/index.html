The work, as delivered

Delivered “Still Cold,” a 30-second script with seven timed scenes, on-screen text and voice-over. The script follows the bottle through everyday use and a next-morning sip, ending with a brand placeholder and call to action. It also includes edit and audio guidance, format assumptions, and notes to verify the performance claim and next-day demonstration before publishing.

  • Vertical 9:16 format with a conversational voice-over.
  • Opening message: “Cold now. Cold tomorrow.”
  • Day 1 and Day 2 labels show the 24-hour transition.
  • Product close-up carries the 24-hour cold-retention claim.
  • Closing frame uses “[Brand]” and “Make the switch.”
  • Notes explain how to handle any required performance qualification.

Summary

A 30-second product video built around one promise: cold drinks, 24 hours later.

The script pairs everyday moments with a clear next-day payoff.

Use the timings below for the edit; replace [Brand] with the bottle’s name before recording.

Video Script — “Still Cold”

Format: Vertical, 9:16

Tone: Fresh, practical, quietly confident

Voice-over: Conversational, with room for natural sound.

TimingScene / VisualOn-screen textVoice-over
0:00–0:03Tight close-up: cold water pours into the open bottle. Cut to the cap twisting shut. Lead with the pour sound.Cold now. Cold tomorrow.“Tomorrow-you deserves a cold drink.”
0:03–0:07Bottle on a kitchen counter beside keys. A hand picks up both and heads out.DAY 1 · 8:00 AM“Fill it before you head out.”
0:07–0:12Two quick cuts: bottle beside a laptop, then in hand on a lunchtime walk. Match its position between shots.Work. Walk. Repeat.“Through work, walks, and everything in between…”
0:12–0:17Return to the same kitchen counter the next morning. Match the earlier framing. The same person opens the bottle and takes a sip.DAY 2 · 8:00 AM“…come back tomorrow. Still cold.”
0:17–0:23Clean product close-up. Slowly move around the bottle, keeping its shape and finish prominent.Keeps drinks cold for 24 hours*“Twenty-four hours of cold, in a bottle made to use again.”
0:23–0:27Fresh water flows into the bottle. Cap on; hand carries it out of frame.Refill. Head out. Repeat.“Refill it. Take it with you.”
0:27–0:30Hero shot against a simple background. Bottle centered, brand and call to action clearly visible.[Brand]<br>Make the switch.“[Brand]. Make the switch.”

Edit & Audio

  • Use a light, steady beat beneath the voice-over. Let the opening pour and cap twist stand out.
  • Make the next-day transition unmistakable with matching camera angles and prominent day labels.
  • Hold the final frame for the full three seconds so the brand and call to action land.

Notes

  • Assumed a general-purpose reusable bottle, a vertical social placement, and no supplied brand assets.
  • The 24-hour claim comes from the brief. Confirm it matches the product’s tested performance before publishing.
  • The next-day scene implies the same water remained cold without a refill. Ensure that demonstration is accurate.
  • * marks any required performance qualification. Replace it with approved wording, displayed legibly alongside the claim, or remove it if no qualification is needed.
