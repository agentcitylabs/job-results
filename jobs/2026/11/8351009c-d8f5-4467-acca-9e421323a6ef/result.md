# Restaurant Website Maintenance: Discovery Call Script

*Assumption: This is a scheduled, 10-minute call with the restaurant owner or manager.*

## Step 1: Open the Conversation

“Hi [Name], thanks for making a few minutes. I know running [Restaurant] keeps you busy, so I’ll keep this short.

“I help small restaurants keep their websites working and up to date—things like menus, opening hours, and reservation links—without adding another job to the manager’s day.

“I’d like to understand how you handle that now, then see whether ongoing maintenance would be useful. Sound good?”

## Step 2: Ask Five Questions

1. **“When your menu, prices, or hours change, who updates the website—and how quickly can they get it done?”**

2. **“Have customers recently mentioned anything confusing or broken on the site, like an old menu or an ordering link that didn’t work?”**

3. **“What’s the most important thing you want someone visiting your website to do: book a table, place an order, or find the information they need to visit?”**

4. **“Who currently checks that the site is working on phones and handles technical updates, backups, or fixes when something goes wrong?”**

5. **“If you could take one website headache off your plate, what would it be—and what monthly budget would feel reasonable for that help?”**

*Let them finish each answer. Use their examples in the close rather than listing every service you offer.*

## Step 3: Recap and Close

**If there’s a fit:**

“From what you’ve said, the main issue is [specific problem], and it means [impact they described]. You’d rather have [desired outcome] than keep dealing with it yourself.

“I’d suggest a maintenance plan focused on [two relevant tasks]. I can send you a short proposal with the monthly price, exactly what’s included, and turnaround times, so you can see whether it makes sense.

“Would [day/time] work for a quick follow-up to review it?”

**If they’re interested but hesitant:**

“That’s fair—you don’t need another expense without a clear reason. I can send a brief recap of the issue we discussed and the cost to handle it. You can review it without committing. Shall I send that to [email]?”

**If there isn’t a current need:**

“It sounds like you’ve got this covered for now. Thanks for walking me through it. If keeping the site updated starts taking too much of your time, I’d be happy to help.”
