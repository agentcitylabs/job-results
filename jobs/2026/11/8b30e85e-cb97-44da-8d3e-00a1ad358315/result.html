SOP-ONB-01: New Hire First Day — Small Design Studio

Document owner: Studio Manager

Version: 1.0

Applies to: All full-time and contract design hires, in-studio or remote

Day-of timing: Based on a 9:00–17:00 studio day. Adjust times to your actual hours.

1. Purpose

Get a new hire oriented, set up with tools, and connected to the team without making them sit through a generic HR deck. The goal is that by 17:00 they have touched one real project, know who to ask for help, and can find the bathroom without asking twice.

2. Roles

RoleWho it isResponsibility
Studio Manager (SM)Office manager, people ops, or studio coordinatorOwns the day, prepares the space and paperwork, removes blockers
Design Lead (DL)Hiring manager, usually a senior designer or creative directorSets project context, assigns first task, checks in on fit and clarity
BuddyAssigned peer, same discipline or one level upSocial anchor, tool walkthrough, lunch, debrief
New Hire (NH)The person startingShows up, asks questions, completes setup and first task

If you are a very small studio, the Studio Manager and Design Lead may be the same person. In that case, the Buddy should take over the physical welcome and tour so the new hire still sees two friendly faces before lunch.

3. Materials to Prepare Before 9:00

  • [ ] Desk cleaned, chair adjusted, monitor/keyboard/mouse tested
  • [ ] Welcome kit: notebook, pen, water bottle, agency sticker pack, one small non-corporate item such as a local coffee card
  • [ ] Badge or key card activated
  • [ ] Payroll, tax, and direct deposit forms printed or sent electronically
  • [ ] Emergency contact form ready
  • [ ] Accounts created and tested: email, Slack, Figma, Adobe Creative Cloud, password manager, time tracking tool
  • [ ] Shared drive and project folders shared with correct permissions
  • [ ] Calendar invite with the day’s schedule sent to NH, SM, DL, and Buddy
  • [ ] Lunch plan confirmed — reservation, order, or delivery
  • [ ] Day 2 plan drafted

4. First Day Steps

TimeStepOwnerWhat to do / say
8:30–8:50Pre-arrival checkSMUnlock studio, turn on lights, set out welcome kit, test logins one final time. Send a Slack message to the team: “New hire arrives at 9 — please be ready to say hi.”
9:00–9:15Arrival and welcomeSMGreet NH at the door. Offer coffee or tea. Walk them to their desk. Point out restrooms, kitchen, fire exits, and where to put a lunch. Do not hand them a 40-page handbook yet.
9:15–9:45Paperwork and account setupSM + IT/OpsComplete payroll, tax, emergency contact, and policy sign-off. Have NH log in to email, Slack, password manager, and time tracking. Verify they can access the shared drive. If any account fails, Buddy takes NH on a studio tour while SM fixes it.
9:45–10:15Team introductionsBuddyWalk NH around the studio or through a short video call. Introduce each person with one concrete line: “Mira leads brand identity. She’s the reason our packaging work doesn’t look like everyone else’s.” Add NH to Slack channels: #general, #design, #random, and any active project channels.
10:15–11:00Project and file overviewDLGive a 30-minute walkthrough of active projects. Use one real client example. Show the file structure, naming conventions, and where work lives. Open a live Figma file and point out what “good” looks like. Grant NH view or comment access to relevant files.
11:00–11:30Brand and process basicsBuddyShow brand guidelines, design system, templates, and the project management board. Explain how to log time daily, where to ask questions, and how to request assets. Keep this practical, not a lecture.
11:30–12:30LunchBuddyBuddy takes NH to lunch, agency pays. First 20 minutes: no shop talk. Then answer questions the NH was too polite to ask in the studio.
12:30–13:30First small taskDLAssign a low-stakes, useful task. Examples: audit one section of a Figma file for naming inconsistencies, update component labels, or read a client brief and write three clarifying questions. The point is to touch real work without pressure.
13:30–14:00Check-in with DLDL15-minute one-on-one. Ask: What made sense? What felt unclear? What do you need tomorrow? Listen more than you talk.
14:00–15:00Shadow or observeBuddyInclude NH in a meeting or design review as an observer. Give context before the meeting and debrief for five minutes after. NH should not present or take notes unless asked.
15:00–15:30Studio ops and normsSMCover communication norms, meeting etiquette, file saving rules, time off, expenses, and security basics. Show where the first aid kit and emergency contacts live.
15:30–16:00Workspace setupNHNH adjusts desk, monitor, chair, downloads fonts, sets Slack notifications, and bookmarks key links: project board, brand hub, shared drive, time tracking. Buddy stays nearby for quick questions.
16:00–16:30End-of-day recapSM + Buddy + NHReview the checklist together. Collect feedback on what would have made the day smoother. Confirm next day start time and first task.
16:30–17:00Wrap-upSMNH reads the onboarding doc and signs off. SM sends a welcome email with links, the Day 2 plan, and a note that the Buddy will check in the next morning.

6. If Something Goes Wrong

  • Account access breaks: Buddy shadows NH on design work or file review while SM fixes access. Do not make NH sit idle.
  • Design Lead is unavailable: Buddy covers the project overview using the DL’s prepared notes. DL reschedules the check-in for the next morning.
  • New hire is remote: Replace the studio tour with a screen-share tour of your digital spaces — Slack channels, Figma projects, shared drive, and project board. Ship the welcome kit in advance. Keep the same order and timing.
