THE SCRIPT

SCENE 1 — THE HOOK

0:00 – 0:03 (3s)

VISUAL:

Extreme macro, top-down. A single ice cube drops into the open mouth of the bottle in slow motion. Hard rim light, dark background, water droplets flaring like sparks. The cube hits with a satisfying clack and a small burst of condensation vapor.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:

7:00 AM

(Small, lower-left, monospace, white, 60% opacity. This timestamp is our throughline — it returns in every scene.)

VO:

"One ice cube. One ordinary Tuesday."

AUDIO: Deep, dry clack of ice on steel. A soft low swell begins underneath. No music yet — let the ice land in near-silence.

NOTE: Shoot at 240fps, cut on the impact frame. This is the thumb-stopper; it has to feel cold in under one second.

SCENE 2 — THE DAY BEGINS

0:03 – 0:09 (6s)

VISUAL:

Three fast handheld beats, roughly 2 seconds each:

  1. The bottle gets clipped into a tote bag side pocket as someone walks out a front door, morning light raking across the floor.
  2. The bottle standing on a desk beside a laptop, out of focus hands typing behind it. Camera pushes in slowly. Condensation is forming — visible beads.
  3. The bottle in a bike bottle cage, shot low and moving, pavement blurring past.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:

8:42 AM
11:15 AM
1:30 PM

(Each timestamp cuts in with its matching shot, same lower-left position. Hard cut, no animation — they should feel like a stopwatch, not a title card.)

VO:

"It goes to work. It goes to lunch. It goes wherever the day goes —"

AUDIO: Music enters on the first cut: a clean, mid-tempo pulse, light percussion. Footsteps, keyboard clatter, and bike-chain hum ducked low under the VO.

NOTE: Keep the lead's face mostly out of frame or soft — this isn't a casting spot, it's a bottle spot. The bottle is the only thing in sharp focus in all three shots.

SCENE 3 — THE PROOF

0:09 – 0:16 (7s)

VISUAL:

Hero product beauty sequence on a clean seamless backdrop, slow 180° orbit. The bottle is sweating on the outside and the lid is off, so we can see down into the neck: the ice is still there, still sharp-edged, floating in clear water.

Then a generative overlay — this is my favorite second of the spot — a thin animated line drawing wraps the bottle: a cutaway of the double wall, with tiny particles bouncing off the vacuum layer instead of through it. Built in code, not stock: the particles should react to the orbit, trailing slightly behind the camera move.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:

DOUBLE-WALL VACUUM INSULATION
(small, kerned wide, appears as the line drawing completes)

VO:

"— and twenty-four hours later, there's still ice in it."

AUDIO: Music drops to just the pulse for a beat under "still ice in it," then the full arrangement returns. A fine, glassy shimmer on the line-drawing reveal.

NOTE: The ice has to be real and genuinely old in this shot. Shoot it last, after a real 24-hour hold, and let the cube be a little rounded. A perfect cube reads as CGI and kills the claim.

SCENE 4 — THE PAYOFF

0:16 – 0:23 (7s)

VISUAL:

Night. Warm, low light — a kitchen counter, or a porch. Same bottle, same day. Hands tip it, and water pours into a glass; one surviving ice cube slides out with it and clinks against the side. Steam-free, cold-clear water. Camera is still, locked off, patient — the first calm shot of the film.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:

7:00 AM (ghosted, 30% opacity)
→ 7:00 AM (full opacity, appears beside it)

FULL VOICE-OVER (clean read, for the booth)

One ice cube. One ordinary Tuesday.
It goes to work. It goes to lunch. It goes wherever the day goes —
— and twenty-four hours later, there's still ice in it.
Fill it once. Forget about it. Everything else can wait.
[PRODUCT], by [BRAND]. Cold all day — guaranteed.

Direction for the VO artist: Close, warm, slightly dry. Think someone telling you about a good thing they found, not someone selling it. The dash after "wherever the day goes" is a real breath — hold it for a half-beat before "and twenty-four hours later." That pause is where the claim lands. Hit "still" in "still ice in it," not "ice."

ONE SMALL THING I'D FIGHT FOR

Don't score the first three seconds. Every bottle ad on the feed opens with a music drop, and they all sound like each other. Opening on a near-silent clack of ice on steel is strange enough that people turn their sound up — and once they've done that, they're yours for the remaining twenty-seven seconds.

That's the whole trick. Cold is a sound before it's a number.
