# Four-week newsletter launch plan for a local bakery

**Assumptions:** The bakery has an owner or manager, one staff member who can help with content, and an existing website or social account. The newsletter will start monthly, using a simple email platform. Launch means sending the first issue to people who have opted in—not uploading every past customer.

**Target:** Send the first newsletter near the end of Week 4, with a working signup process and the next issue scheduled.

Times below are **hands-on estimates**, not waiting time. One person can cover several roles.

## Week 1 — Decide what to send and set up the basics

### 1. Agree on the newsletter’s purpose
- **Job:** Choose the audience, main goal and monthly sending rhythm. Keep the promise specific: “A monthly note with seasonal bakes, bakery news and preorder dates.”
- **Deliverable:** A short outline naming the audience, content promise, main action readers should take and launch date.
- **Who:** Owner or manager, with a marketing helper.
- **Time:** 1 hour.
- **Order:** First; this guides everything else.

### 2. Choose an email platform and create the account
- **Job:** Compare two suitable platforms on price, signup forms, ease of use and unsubscribe handling. Set up the chosen account with a recognizable sender name and reply address.
- **Deliverable:** An account owned by the bakery, with appropriate staff access.
- **Who:** Marketing helper or web freelancer; owner approves cost.
- **Time:** 1–2 hours.
- **Order:** After Task 1.

### 3. Set up permission, privacy and delivery basics
- **Job:** Check whether any existing contacts have documented permission to receive marketing. Exclude those without it. Configure unsubscribe details, required business information and a short privacy notice appropriate to local requirements. Authenticate the sending domain if available.
- **Deliverable:** A documented opt-in process and a configured sender account. Use the platform’s verified-sender guidance if there is no bakery domain.
- **Who:** Owner and marketing helper; web support for domain settings.
- **Time:** 2–3 hours, plus any verification wait.
- **Order:** After Task 2; before collecting or importing subscribers.

## Week 2 — Start collecting subscribers and build the format

### 4. Publish and test the signup form
- **Job:** Create a mobile-friendly form asking for email address only, with first name optional. State what subscribers will receive and how often. Enable confirmation emails where appropriate.
- **Deliverable:** A live signup link, added to the website if available and the bakery’s social profile. Test signup, confirmation and unsubscribe.
- **Who:** Marketing helper or web freelancer.
- **Time:** 1.5–2 hours.
- **Order:** After Task 3.

### 5. Promote signup in the shop and online
- **Job:** Make a counter sign with a QR code and a clear reason to join. Publish one social announcement and prepare a friendly staff invitation: “Want our monthly seasonal menu and preorder dates? You can sign up here.”
- **Deliverable:** Printed sign, social post and a short staff briefing. Joining stays optional; staff should not add customers without permission.
- **Who:** Front-of-house lead, with marketing help.
- **Time:** 1.5–2 hours setup, then 10 minutes per week to check materials and reminders.
- **Order:** After Task 4. Keep promotion running through launch.

### 6. Build one reusable email template
- **Job:** Create a simple, mobile-friendly layout with the bakery’s logo, one main photo, short sections, a clear button and the required footer. Use readable text, descriptive links and image descriptions.
- **Deliverable:** A reusable template with room for a seasonal feature, a bakery note and one main call to action.
- **Who:** Marketing helper or email designer.
- **Time:** 2–3 hours.
- **Order:** After Task 2; can run alongside Tasks 4–5.

## Week 3 — Make the first issue

### 7. Gather launch content and photos
- **Job:** Select a seasonal bake, a short behind-the-scenes story and useful dates or ordering information. Take two or three clear photos in natural light.
- **Deliverable:** A small content folder with approved facts, prices, availability, images and links.
- **Who:** Baker or owner provides details; a staff member takes photos.
- **Time:** 1.5–2 hours.
- **Order:** After Task 1; use current information close to launch.

### 8. Write and assemble the first issue
- **Job:** Draft roughly 250–400 words in the bakery’s voice. Add a subject line, preview text and one primary action, such as viewing the seasonal menu or placing a preorder. Put the copy and photos into the template.
- **Deliverable:** A complete draft email.
- **Who:** Marketing helper or freelance writer.
- **Time:** 2–3 hours.
- **Order:** After Tasks 6–7.

### 9. Check the customer journey and shop readiness
- **Job:** Follow the email’s main link as a customer would. Confirm ordering works, dates and prices match, and staff can handle likely questions. If there is an offer, check margin, stock limits, expiry and redemption instructions.
- **Deliverable:** A working destination page or clear ordering method, plus staff notes.
- **Who:** Owner or manager, with web support if needed.
- **Time:** 1 hour.
- **Order:** After Task 8; before approval.

## Week 4 — Test, send and learn

### 10. Test and approve the email
- **Job:** Send previews to two people. Check on a phone and desktop: spelling, images, links, sender details, reply address and unsubscribe. Confirm dates, prices and availability again.
- **Deliverable:** An approved final email and a completed test checklist.
- **Who:** Marketing helper runs tests; owner signs off.
- **Time:** 1–1.5 hours.
- **Order:** After Tasks 8–9. Leave at least one working day to fix problems.

### 11. Send the first newsletter
- **Job:** Review the opted-in audience, remove test contacts and choose a send time when someone can monitor replies. Schedule or send the issue.
- **Deliverable:** First newsletter sent, with replies and ordering questions checked that day.
- **Who:** Marketing helper sends; owner or front-of-house lead monitors responses.
- **Time:** 45 minutes–1 hour.
- **Order:** After Task 10, ideally around Day 25 so there is time for an initial review.

### 12. Review results and book the next issue
- **Job:** After 48–72 hours, record delivered emails, clicks, unsubscribes, replies and any trackable orders or redemptions. Treat open rates as directional because privacy features can distort them. Note one thing to keep and one thing to improve.
- **Deliverable:** A short results note, next issue date, topic and named owner.
- **Who:** Marketing helper and owner.
- **Time:** 1 hour.
- **Order:** After Task 11, by the end of Week 4.

## Workload and priorities

**Estimated total:** About **18–24 hands-on hours**, plus brief signup-promotion checks and any technical verification delays. Platform fees, printing and any paid specialist support are separate.

**Critical path:** Purpose → platform → permission and sender setup → signup → draft → test → send.

**If time gets tight:** Keep one template, one strong photo and one main action. Skip discounts, complex automation and elaborate design. Do not skip consent checks, link testing or owner approval. A small list of genuinely interested neighbors is a good starting point.
