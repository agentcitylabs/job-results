# Discovery Call Script: Website Maintenance for Small Restaurants

**Assumption:** This is a scheduled 10–15-minute call with the restaurant’s owner or manager. Replace bracketed details before use.

## Opening

“Hi [Name], thanks for making time. I know you’ve got a restaurant to run, so I’ll keep this to about ten minutes.

“I help small restaurants keep their websites accurate and working—from menus and opening hours to booking and ordering links.

“I’d like to understand how you handle the site now and whether anything’s creating extra work or getting in the way of customers. If there’s a fit, I’ll suggest a simple maintenance option. If not, I’ll say so.”

## Five Discovery Questions

**1. “Who looks after your website today, and what happens when you need something changed?”**  
*Listen for:* An owner doing updates after hours, a hard-to-reach freelancer, or no clear person responsible.

**2. “When a customer lands on your site, what’s the most important thing you want them to do—book a table, order food, find your menu, or come in?”**  
*Listen for:* The customer action maintenance needs to protect. Don’t assume every restaurant wants online orders.

**3. “In the last few months, has anything on the site caused confusion or failed to work—like old prices, incorrect hours, or a broken booking link?”**  
*Listen for:* Specific incidents and consequences: extra phone calls, frustrated guests, staff correcting information, or possible missed business.

**4. “How often do you change menus, prices, hours, or special offers, and how quickly do those updates need to go live?”**  
*Listen for:* The likely workload and turnaround needed. Holiday hours and seasonal menus often matter more than frequent redesigns.

**5. “If someone took this off your plate, what would make a monthly service worth paying for—and what monthly budget would feel comfortable?”**  
*Listen for:* Their definition of value, a realistic budget, and whether they want routine help or broader improvements.

## Close the Call

### Reflect back what you heard

“Let me make sure I’ve got this right. Your main priority is [bookings/orders/accurate information]. Right now, [specific problem] means [specific consequence]. You’d like [desired outcome] without having to chase someone or do it yourself.”

*Pause for confirmation.*

### Recommend a focused next step

“Based on that, I’d suggest maintenance focused on [two or three relevant tasks—for example, menu updates, holiday hours, and checks of your booking links].

“Before quoting, I’d review the public site and confirm the platform and access needed. Then I’d send a short proposal with the monthly price, exactly what’s included, update turnaround times, and what would cost extra. A redesign or fixing the booking provider’s own system would be separate.”

### Ask for a clear commitment

“Would you like me to do that review and send the proposal by [day]? We can set aside ten minutes on [day/time] to go through it and decide whether it makes sense.”

### If they’re not ready

“No problem. I’ll send a brief recap of what we discussed so you have it in one place. Would a follow-up before [their next menu change/busy season] be useful, or would you prefer to leave it with you?”

### If there isn’t a maintenance fit

“It sounds like ongoing maintenance may be more than you need right now. The immediate priority is [specific fix]. I’d start there rather than add a monthly bill.”
