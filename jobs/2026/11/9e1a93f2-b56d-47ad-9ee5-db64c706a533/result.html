Notion vs. Obsidian vs. Apple Notes

For a student taking lecture notes on both a laptop and a phone, the key trade-offs are how easily notes sync, how quickly you can capture them, and how well each tool works across different devices.

ToolWhat works well for studentsMain trade-off
NotionSyncs across major platforms, so it’s a dependable choice if your laptop and phone aren’t from the same company. Pages, databases, and search make it easy to organize notes by course, week, or assignment.It can feel like more setup than you need for plain lecture notes. Offline use is less dependable than with local-first apps, so don’t assume every page will be available without a connection.
ObsidianNotes are stored as plain-text Markdown files on your device, making them portable and available offline. Links between notes are useful for connecting ideas across lectures and courses.Phone–laptop syncing may require a paid service or some configuration. Its flexibility is powerful, but setting up folders, links, and plugins can become a project of its own.
Apple NotesVery quick for capturing and finding notes, with smooth syncing across Apple devices. It’s a particularly low-friction option for a student using a Mac and iPhone.It’s less convenient on non-Apple devices, and its organization and customization are simpler than Notion’s or Obsidian’s.

Recommendation: Notion

Assuming you want one tool that works across a laptop and phone, without knowing that both are Apple devices, I’d choose Notion. You can keep a straightforward course page for each class, add lecture notes as you go, and find them later from either device. It also leaves room to track readings, assignments, and deadlines alongside your notes.

Keep the setup simple: make one page per course and one page per lecture. If you often take notes somewhere with unreliable internet, check that you can access the material you need offline—or keep a backup for important lectures.

Choose Apple Notes instead if you use a Mac and iPhone and want the quickest, simplest capture. Choose Obsidian if offline access, local files, and connecting ideas across notes matter more than effortless setup and syncing.
