# SOP-001: First Day Onboarding — New Designer
**Agency:** Studio North  
**Effective Date:** [Date]  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Office Manager & Hiring Manager

---

## Purpose
To make a new hire's first day feel seamless, human, and productive. No scrambling for logins, no awkward silences, and no "figure it out yourself" moments. By 5:00 PM, the new designer should know their team, their tools, and their first assignment.

## Assumptions
- The offer letter is signed and background checks are complete.
- The new hire has been given the start date and location at least one week in advance.
- The agency has between 5 and 15 employees (small team culture).

---

## The 13 Steps

### Step 1: Pre-Arrival Workspace Setup
**Owner:** Office Manager  
**When:** The day before the start date.

Set up the new hire's physical workstation. This includes a clean desk, a monitor, a keyboard, a mouse, and a working power strip. Place a printed welcome note and a Studio North branded mug on the desk. If the hire is remote, ship the hardware a week early and test the video call setup.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Desk assigned and cleaned
- [ ] Monitor, keyboard, and mouse tested
- [ ] Welcome note and mug placed
- [ ] Remote kit shipped (if applicable)

---

### Step 2: Greeting & Office Tour
**Owner:** Founder / Office Manager  
**When:** 9:00 AM sharp.

Meet the new hire at the front door. Spend 10 minutes on a physical tour. Point out the kitchen, the bathrooms, the quiet room, and the good coffee machine (the one in the back is faster). Explain the door code or issue a keycard. If remote, do a 10-minute virtual walkthrough of the digital "office" (Notion, Drive, etc.).

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Welcome at the door
- [ ] Physical or virtual tour completed
- [ ] Keycard or door code issued
- [ ] Coffee machine location pointed out

---

### Step 3: Administrative Paperwork
**Owner:** Admin / Office Manager  
**When:** 9:15 AM – 9:45 AM.

Sit down together and knock out the boring but essential stuff: tax forms, direct deposit details, and emergency contacts. Briefly review the employee handbook, especially the remote work policy and the paid time off structure. Keep it light—offer a coffee or tea during this.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Tax forms completed
- [ ] Direct deposit set up
- [ ] Emergency contacts collected
- [ ] Employee handbook received and acknowledged

---

### Step 4: Hardware & Software Provisioning
**Owner:** IT / Lead Designer  
**When:** 9:45 AM – 10:30 AM.

Install and license all essential software. For a design agency, that means Figma, Adobe Creative Cloud, Slack, Notion, and Linear (or your project management tool of choice). Ensure the password manager is set up and the laptop is configured with the correct display scaling (designers care about this).

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Laptop configured and updated
- [ ] Figma and Adobe CC licensed
- [ ] Slack and Notion installed
- [ ] Password manager initialized
- [ ] PM tool (Linear/Asana) access granted

---

### Step 5: Access & Account Creation
**Owner:** IT / Admin  
**When:** 10:30 AM – 11:00 AM.

Create the company email address. Send invites to Slack, Notion, and any client-specific tools like Loom or Miro. Grant access to the relevant Google Drive folders—specifically the "Active Projects" folder and the "Brand Assets" folder. Do not give access to the "Old_Projects_2022" folder yet; it's a trap.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Company email created
- [ ] Slack invite sent and accepted
- [ ] Notion workspace shared
- [ ] Drive access granted to active projects
- [ ] Client tool accounts created

---

### Step 6: Communication Norms
**Owner:** Project Manager  
**When:** 11:00 AM – 11:20 AM.

Walk through Slack. Explain the channel structure: `#general`, `#design-crit`, `#project-updates`, and `#random`. Establish the golden rules: "If it's urgent, tag @here. If it's a design critique, post in #design-crit, not in a DM." Set their Do Not Disturb hours to match the team's deep-work block (usually 1:00 PM – 3:00 PM).

**Checklist:**
- [ ] All relevant Slack channels joined
- [ ] DND hours configured
- [ ] Recurring meeting invites sent (stand-up, crit, etc.)
- [ ] Communication norms explained

---

### Step 7: Team Introductions (Round Robin)
**Owner:** Hiring Manager  
**When:** 11:20 AM – 11:50 AM.

Gather the team (5–6 people) for a quick 15-minute intro session. Keep it structured: each person says their name, their role, and one fun fact. The Hiring Manager should explicitly state who handles what—"If you need client files, ask Maya. If the printer is jammed, ask Tom."

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Met all team members
- [ ] Knows the go-to person for client files
- [ ] Knows the go-to person for IT issues
- [ ] Knows who to ask for creative feedback

---

### Step 8: Buddy Assignment & Lunch
**Owner:** Buddy (Senior Designer)  
**When:** 12:00 PM – 1:30 PM.

Assign a buddy—a peer, not a manager. The buddy takes the new hire to lunch (or orders in, if remote). During lunch, the buddy shares the "unwritten rules" of the studio. Examples: "We never book the meeting room on Fridays," "The plant in the corner is named Gerald and he is sacred," and "Crits are for feedback, not for egos."

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Buddy introduced
- [ ] Lunch scheduled and completed
- [ ] Unwritten rules shared
- [ ] Buddy's contact info exchanged

---

### Step 9: Client & Project Context
**Owner:** Account Manager / Project Manager  
**When:** 1:30 PM – 2:15 PM.

Review the current active projects. Show the client roster and explain the personality of each client. For example: "Client A likes fast turnarounds but hates surprises. Client B wants to see three options every time." Highlight upcoming deadlines and which projects are in the "quiet phase" versus the "crunch phase."

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Active project briefs shared
- [ ] Client roster reviewed
- [ ] Upcoming deadlines highlighted
- [ ] Client communication preferences explained

---

### Step 10: Design Tools & Brand Guidelines
**Owner:** Senior Designer  
**When:** 2:15 PM – 3:00 PM.

Walk through the shared asset library. Show where the master fonts live, where the logo files are, and how to access the color palette. Explain the file naming convention (e.g., `client_project_v2_final_FINAL` is forbidden; instead use `client_project_2024-05-01`). Open the brand guidelines PDF and point out the "do not stretch the logo" section.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Access to asset library verified
- [ ] Brand guidelines opened and reviewed
- [ ] File naming convention explained
- [ ] Shared drive structure understood

---

### Step 11: First Assignment Briefing
**Owner:** Creative Director  
**When:** 3:00 PM – 3:45 PM.

Give the new hire a small, low-pressure task. Something like: "Create a mood board for an upcoming pitch" or "Update the social media template for Q3." Explain the *why* behind the task—how it fits into the bigger picture. Define the success criteria clearly and set a loose deadline (2–3 days out). This is their first win; make it achievable.

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Task assigned
- [ ] Success criteria defined
- [ ] Deadline set
- [ ] Context for the task provided

---

### Step 12: Culture & Rituals
**Owner:** Project Manager / Founder  
**When:** 3:45 PM – 4:15 PM.

Explain the weekly rhythm: Monday morning stand-up, Thursday afternoon design crit, Friday show-and-tell. Explain the "no-blame" culture—mistakes are for learning, not for pointing fingers. Briefly touch on company values (e.g., "We ship fast, but we ship kind").

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Weekly schedule reviewed
- [ ] Design crit etiquette explained
- [ ] Company values discussed
- [ ] Stand-up and crit calendar invites confirmed

---

### Step 13: End-of-Day Check-in
**Owner:** Hiring Manager  
**When:** 4:30 PM – 5:00 PM.

Sit down one-on-one. Ask three questions: "What was confusing today?" "What did you enjoy?" and "Do you have access to everything you need?" Take notes on their answers. Set a clear plan for Day 2 and outline the goals for the first week. End on a positive note: "We're really glad you're here."

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Confusions addressed
- [ ] Access issues resolved
- [ ] Day 2 plan set
- [ ] First week goals outlined
- [ ] Positive send-off given
