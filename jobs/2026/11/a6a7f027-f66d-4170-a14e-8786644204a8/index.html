Baseline Metrics & Trajectory

Here is the exact performance breakdown across the first half of the year (January through June):

  • Monthly Sequence: 120 (Jan) → 135 (Feb) → 128 (Mar) → 160 (Apr) → 172 (May) → 158 (Jun)
  • Total Volume: 873 units
  • Six-Month Average (Mean): 145.5 units per month
  • Net Growth (January to June):
  • Absolute Gain: +38 units
  • Percentage Growth: +31.67% ((158 - 120) / 120 * 100)
  • Peak Performance: May (172 units)
  • Trough: January (120 units)

Trend Analysis: The Half-Year Arc

The series shows an active, expanding business with three distinct operational phases:

  1. The Q1 Foundation (Jan–Mar): January opened at 120, followed by a modest lift to 135 in February and a minor consolidation to 128 in March. This represents a steady, range-bound first quarter averaging 127.7 units, establishing your initial operational floor.
  2. The Spring Breakout (Apr–May): Volume broke out sharply in April with a jump of +32 units (+25.0% month-over-month), hitting 160 and expanding further to the six-month high of 172 in May. This two-month window shifted your run-rate into an entirely higher tier.
  3. The June Pullback (Jun): June closed at 158, down 14 units (-8.14%) from May's high water mark. While down from the peak, June remained 8.6% above your six-month baseline (145.5) and substantially higher than any month in Q1. The overarching trend remains distinctly upward, shifting from an early-year baseline around 125–130 into a mid-year range hovering around 160–170.

Two Diagnostic Checks to Run Next

To ensure you interpret this upward trend accurately without masking underlying operational shifts, I recommend running two targeted audits:

1. Price Realization vs. Volume Driver Check

  • What to inspect: Verify whether the breakout across April (160) and May (172) was driven by an increase in net transaction count, larger order sizes, or temporary discounting/promotional incentives.
  • Why it matters: If the spring spike was fueled by price cuts or packaged discounts, your gross sales count rose while margin per deal may have compressed. Confirm that net revenue and gross margin tracked the +31.67% unit trajectory proportionally.

2. June Churn & Pipeline Pull-Forward Audit

  • What to inspect: Examine whether June’s drop to 158 reflects early churn from the accounts acquired during the April–May spike, or if May's peak was artificially inflated by sales reps pulling forward deals scheduled for June to hit mid-quarter targets.
  • Why it matters: If deals were simply accelerated into May, June’s 158 is a healthy normalization. If June reflects churn or delayed onboarding falloff, it signals a fulfillment capacity ceiling that must be addressed before the next growth push.

Notes

  • Unit Basis: Calculations treat all figures as identical unit counts (such as contracts, subscriptions, or physical products). If these values represent denominated revenue (e.g., thousands of dollars), the percentages remain identical, but per-unit interpretations should be adjusted accordingly.
  • Seasonality Assumption: This assessment assumes an unadjusted calendar timeline. If your category typically experiences a natural historical summer dip or second-quarter procurement rush, compare these numbers against the prior year's same-period trajectory (Jan–Jun) to isolate organic momentum from baseline seasonality.
