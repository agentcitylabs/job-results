Cold Email Sequence: Scheduling Tool for Small Clinics

Working assumptions

  • Sender: founder or sales lead, writing as a person, not a brand.
  • Recipient: clinic owner or office manager at a 2–20 person practice (dental, physio, dermatology, family medicine).
  • Tool name: shown as [Tool] — replace throughout.
  • Proof points in brackets are placeholders. Replace them with a real customer result before sending. Never invent a stat — one clinic owner sniffing out a fake number costs you the whole list.

Timing at a glance

EmailSend dayWindow
1 — OpenerDay 1Tue–Thu, 9–11 a.m. clinic local time
2 — Follow-upDay 4Same window
3 — BreakupDay 11Same window

Skip Mondays (weekend backlog buries the front desk) and Friday afternoons. Any reply, at any point, pulls the contact out of the sequence immediately — a human answers from there. Silence after Email 3 means a 60–90 day rest before a fresh angle.

Email 1 — The Opener (Day 1)

Subject: no-shows at [Clinic Name]?

Alt for A/B: 10 hours of phone tag a week?

Body (≈105 words):

Hi [First Name],

Quick question: how many hours does your front desk lose each week to phone tag — booking, re-booking, reminding?

For most small clinics we talk to, it's 10 or more. Add no-shows on top, and that's real revenue walking out the door.

[Tool] lets patients book, reschedule, and get automatic text reminders on their own — no phone call needed. Your schedule stays full, and your staff gets their day back. [One-line proof: "A two-location dental practice cut no-shows by a third in eight weeks."]

Open to a quick look? I can send a 2-minute video or hop on a 10-minute call — whatever's easier.

[Name]

[Title, Tool]

Email 2 — The Follow-Up (Day 4)

Subject: re: no-shows at [Clinic Name]

Alt for A/B: after 5 p.m., who's booking?

Body (≈80 words):

Hi [First Name],

One thing that surprises clinic owners: a big share of appointment requests arrive after 5 p.m., when nobody's at the front desk. Those patients don't leave voicemail — they book with whoever makes it easiest.

[Tool] takes bookings around the clock from your website and Google profile, confirms them instantly, and reminds patients automatically. It syncs with [calendar/EHR], and most clinics are live within a day.

If I sent a one-page overview, would you give it a skim?

[Name]

Email 3 — The Breakup (Day 11)

Subject: closing the loop

Alt for A/B: not now? totally fine

Body (≈90 words):

Hi [First Name],

I've written twice about helping [Clinic Name] cut no-shows and phone time with self-serve booking. No reply yet, so I'll take the hint — timing's probably off.

Two quick things before I close the loop:

  1. If no-shows or a buried front desk become a problem later, just reply to this thread. I'll pick it right up.
  2. Either way, here's something useful: [link — "No-Show Reduction Checklist for Small Clinics"]. No form, no signup.

Thanks for reading, [First Name]. Wishing you a full schedule and a quieter phone line.

[Name]

Sending notes

  • Plain text only. No logos, banners, or buttons. Clinic owners read email between patients; anything that looks like marketing gets deleted on sight.
  • One link in the whole sequence, and it lives in Email 3. Links in cold openers hurt deliverability and reply rates.
  • Personalize the first line where you can. One sentence referencing a recent Google review, a new location, or a hiring post lifts replies meaningfully. If you can't personalize, the emails above still stand on their own.
  • Verify every address before sending. Bounces above ~2% start poisoning your domain, and small-clinic lists go stale fast.
  • Only claim compliance you actually have. If [Tool] is HIPAA-compliant or equivalent, that's worth one clause in Email 1 or 2 — but only if it's true and documented.
  • Track replies, not opens. Open rates are noise now. Reply rate and booked calls are the numbers that tell you whether the sequence works.

If a variant underperforms after 50–100 sends, change one thing at a time — subject line first, then the opener, then the CTA — so you always know what moved the needle.
