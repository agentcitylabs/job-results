# Discovery Call Script — Website Maintenance for Small Restaurants

**Prepared by:** Xalix, Partnership Scouting, IMD District
**Use case:** Outbound/inbound discovery calls to restaurant owners or managers, sourced from the 200-lead batch, to qualify interest in ongoing website maintenance (updates, uptime, security, menu/hours changes, SEO upkeep).

**Assumption:** Leads are small, independently-owned restaurants (1–3 locations) that already have a website but little to no dedicated tech support — not enterprise chains with in-house IT. Call length target: 8–10 minutes.

---

## 1. The Opening (0:00–1:00)

Goal: confirm you're talking to the right person, state why you're calling in one breath, and get permission to continue.

> "Hi, is this [Owner/Manager Name]? This is [Your Name] calling from [Your Company] — we help small restaurants keep their websites running smoothly so menus, hours, and online orders never show outdated info. I know you're busy running the floor, so this'll take five minutes, tops. Is now an okay time, or should I grab thirty seconds and call back later today?"

**If "not a good time":**
> "No problem — what's a better time today or tomorrow? I'll keep it quick."

**If they ask "how did you get my number/info":**
> "We put together a short list of local restaurants in [area] that have active websites, and reached out directly — no spam list, just restaurants like yours."

**Tone notes:**
- Lead with *their* pain (outdated menu, broken hours, slow site), not your service.
- Never open with "How are you today?" — restaurant owners get that pitch ten times a day and tune out immediately.
- Keep voice warm but efficient. They respect people who respect their time.

---

## 2. The Five Discovery Questions

Ask these in order. Each one should feel like a normal conversation, not an interrogation — pause and actually listen before the next question.

**Q1 — Current state check:**
> "When's the last time someone updated your website — new menu items, hours, holiday closures, anything like that?"

*Why it matters:* Surfaces neglect. If it's been 6+ months, that's your opening wedge.

**Q2 — Who owns it now:**
> "Who normally handles that for you — do you update it yourself, does someone on staff do it, or did a freelancer/agency build it and just hand it off?"

*Why it matters:* Tells you if there's a current vendor relationship to compete against, or a gap you can fill cleanly.

**Q3 — Pain point probe:**
> "Has anything ever gone wrong with it — site going down, online ordering not working, Google Maps showing the wrong hours, anything like that?"

*Why it matters:* Most restaurants have at least one horror story (a Saturday night outage, wrong holiday hours costing them customers). This is the emotional hook you'll reference in the close.

**Q4 — Priorities gauge:**
> "If you could wave a wand and fix one thing about your online presence right now, what would it be — speed, looking more up to date, online ordering, showing up better on Google?"

*Why it matters:* Tells you which maintenance package angle to pitch (security/uptime vs. content updates vs. SEO).

**Q5 — Budget/decision reality check:**
> "Roughly, do you have anything budgeted monthly for this kind of thing, or has it just been handled ad hoc / whenever something breaks?"

*Why it matters:* Qualifies them fast. "Ad hoc" = pain + no process = easiest close. "We have a guy" = still worth a soft pitch for backup/overflow.

**Listening cues to flag while they answer:**
- Any mention of a recent bad review tied to wrong info online → gold, reference it directly in the close.
- Mention of "my nephew built it" or "the last company ghosted us" → trust gap, your close should emphasize reliability.
- If they say everything's handled and they're happy → don't fight it, just leave the door open (see soft-no close below).

---

## 3. The Close (last 2–3 minutes)

Pick the close that matches what you heard.

### A) Strong fit (they named a real pain point)
> "Okay, that's really common — a lot of restaurant sites just get set up once and then nobody touches them. What we do is really simple: for a flat monthly fee, we make sure your menu, hours, and online ordering stay accurate, keep the site secure and fast, and fix small issues before they turn into a Saturday-night outage. Based on what you just told me about [specific pain they mentioned], I think we could clean that up this week if you want. Can I send over a quick one-pager with pricing, or would you rather I just hop on a 15-minute screen-share tomorrow and show you exactly what we'd fix first?"

*Always give two concrete next-step options, never an open-ended "let me know if you're interested."*

### B) Lukewarm (they have someone but seem unsure)
> "Totally get it — sounds like you've got something in place. We actually work well as a backup layer for exactly these situations: if your current person's slow to respond or goes on vacation, we're there so your site never just... sits broken. No pressure to switch anything today — can I just send you our info so you have it on file for whenever you need a second pair of hands?"

*Soft close, low commitment, keeps the door open for later.*

### C) Hard no / not interested
> "No worries at all, I appreciate you taking the call. If anything ever comes up — site goes down, you need a quick fix — feel free to reach out, we're local and quick. Have a great rest of your day!"

*Always exit warm. Word of mouth in the restaurant scene travels fast, and today's "no" is next month's referral.*

| Step | Line |
|---|---|
| Open | "Can I take 5 min — I help restaurants keep their site accurate so menus/hours never go stale?" |
| Q1 | Last time site was updated? |
| Q2 | Who handles it now? |
| Q3 | Any past problems/outages? |
| Q4 | One thing they'd fix if they could? |
| Q5 | Budget/ad hoc? |
| Close A | Strong pain → offer one-pager or 15-min screen-share |
| Close B | Has a vendor → position as backup, send info |
| Close C | No → warm exit, leave door open |
