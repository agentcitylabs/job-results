## Pitch Deck Outline

**Slide 1 — Title / Hook**  
**Title:** PawPair: The trusted walk around the corner  
**Key point:** PawPair matches dog owners with vetted, nearby walkers for same-day and recurring walks—so the dog gets out, even when life doesn’t cooperate.

**Slide 2 — Problem**  
**Title:** Dog owners love their dogs. They don’t love the 6 p.m. scramble.  
**Key point:** Busy owners regularly skip or delay walks because finding a walker is slow, untrustworthy, or too expensive. The result is guilty owners and under-exercised dogs.

**Slide 3 — Current Options Fail**  
**Title:** Today’s choices are impersonal apps or a stranger from a Facebook group.  
**Key point:** National apps feel faceless and often have thin local availability. Neighborhood boards are unvetted and unreliable. Neither gives an owner confidence when handing over a house key and a beloved dog.

**Slide 4 — Solution**  
**Title:** A local marketplace built around trust and routine  
**Key point:** PawPair shows vetted walkers within walking distance, lets owners book a one-off walk or set a weekly schedule, and gives live updates from the walk.

**Slide 5 — How It Works for Owners**  
**Title:** Book a walk in under 60 seconds  
**Key point:** The owner enters an address and dog profile, sees available local walkers with reviews and repeat-client badges, books and pays in-app, then follows the walk on a map.

**Slide 6 — How It Works for Walkers**  
**Title:** Walkers earn on their own schedule, close to home  
**Key point:** Approved walkers set their radius, availability, and rates. They accept jobs that fit their day and get paid weekly—no chasing invoices.

**Slide 7 — Trust and Safety**  
**Title:** Trust is the product  
**Key point:** Every walker is identity-checked, trained, and reviewed. Every walk has GPS tracking, photo updates, and insurance coverage—so handing over the leash feels safe.

**Slide 8 — Market Opportunity**  
**Title:** Dog care is a large, local, recurring market  
**Key point:** Urban dog ownership is growing, and walking is the highest-frequency pet service. PawPair starts in one dense neighborhood and expands city by city.

**Slide 9 — Business Model**  
**Title:** Simple take rate, strong repeat usage  
**Key point:** PawPair charges owners a transparent per-walk fee and takes a percentage from each booking. Recurring schedules and local trust drive high retention and low acquisition cost.

**Slide 10 — Ask / Traction**  
**Title:** We’re raising [amount] to own the first city  
**Key point:** With seed funding, PawPair will recruit and vet 100 walkers, launch in [city/neighborhood], and reach [X] weekly walks in 12 months—then replicate the playbook.

## Notes

- I used **PawPair** as a working name. Replace it with the actual app name if one exists.
- I left bracketed placeholders for the raise amount, launch city, and traction target. Fill those with real numbers before presenting.
- I did not invent market statistics. If you have local data on dog ownership, walk frequency, or competitor gaps, add it to slides 2, 3, and 8.
- The outline assumes a seed-stage investor pitch, but it can also work for city partnerships or accelerator applications with small wording changes.
- Each slide is kept to one key point so the deck survives a fast read. Use visuals—screenshots, maps, walker profiles—to support the point, not repeat it.
