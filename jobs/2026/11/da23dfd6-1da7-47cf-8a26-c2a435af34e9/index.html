The work, as delivered

The deliverable presents three written logo concepts: Cup as Sun, Bean Trajectory and Cart Halo. Each includes a concept story, shape description, two-colour palette with hex codes, and a font pairing. It also describes small-size simplifications and suggests testing the marks as 16-pixel favicons and 8 cm vinyl applications.

  • Cup as Sun combines a cup rim, coffee disk and tilted orbital ring.
  • Cup as Sun pairs #2C1810 and #E8B86D with Fraunces and DM Sans.
  • Bean Trajectory uses a rotated coffee bean with an S-curve crease extending into a partial orbit.
  • Bean Trajectory pairs #3D2B1F and #F0E6D2 with Source Serif 4 and Outfit.
  • Cart Halo places a simplified side-view cart inside an incomplete orbital ring.
  • Cart Halo pairs #1B3A4B and #E07A5F with Space Grotesk and Lora italic.

Morning Orbit — Three Logo Concepts

I drew these as if they had to survive at sixteen pixels on a cart chalkboard, a paper cup, and a tiny map pin. Morning Orbit is a moving thing: the first loop of the day, a cup that puts you in motion. I assumed a small independent cart (pour-over or compact espresso, parks and weekday corners) with no existing colours, so every mark is two inks only, high contrast, and cuttable as vinyl or a rubber stamp.

Concept 1: Cup as Sun

Idea. The cart is the neighbourhood sun. People orbit it for the first cup; the day starts when the cart does. The mark is both a top-down coffee and a small star — one shape doing two jobs so it never feels fussy on a moving cart.

Shapes. A heavy outer circle (cup rim / solar disk). Inside it, a slightly offset smaller circle for the coffee surface, leaving a crescent of negative space like early light. A single thin elliptical ring crosses the whole thing at a gentle 20-degree tilt — steam, orbit path, and the cart’s daily route in one stroke. At 16 px the ellipse becomes a simple oval halo; the inner circle stays a solid punch of colour. No extra details.

Palette. #2C1810 (almost-black roast, the cup and type) and #E8B86D (honey gold, the orbit ring and coffee disk). The gold sits warm against the dark without going neon; it still reads on kraft paper.

Font pairing. Wordmark in Fraunces (optical size 72, slightly soft serifs, the warmth of a ceramic mug). Any supporting line (“coffee cart” or hours) in DM Sans medium — geometric enough to stay crisp at small sizes, never competing with the serif.

Concept 2: Bean Trajectory

Idea. One coffee bean whose crease is already an orbit. Once you have the cup, your morning has a path. Tactile and a little agricultural — it feels like it could be stamped on a sleeve or burned into a wooden cart panel.

Shapes. A classic bean oval, rotated 15 degrees so it isn’t dead-on. The central crease is a smooth S-curve that deliberately overshoots the bean on both ends and continues into a partial outer ring (about 240 degrees). Negative space between bean and ring keeps it light. At 16 px you drop the overshoot and keep only the bean plus a short arc; it still reads as “coffee in motion.”

Palette. #3D2B1F (earth-brown bean) and #F0E6D2 (oat-milk cream for the ring and lettering). High contrast, works on both dark cart paint and pale cups. The cream never looks dirty.

Font pairing. Name set in Source Serif 4 (regular, old-style figures if you ever add a year). Tagline or “mobile” in Outfit semibold — a clean geometric sans that echoes the oval geometry without looking techy.

Concept 3: Cart Halo

Idea. This one owns the “cart” part. It is not a café; it is a small vehicle that loops the morning. Wheels become orbits, the whole thing sits inside a halo like a planet on a short elliptical path through the neighbourhood.

Shapes. Side-view cart reduced to four pieces: a low rectangle (body), two solid circles (wheels), a tiny rectangle on top for the service hatch. A third, larger incomplete circle (270 degrees) wraps the entire cart like a halo or orbital ring, starting from the back wheel and opening toward the front — the direction of travel. Steam is a single short arc above the hatch; at 16 px you omit steam and keep only body + wheels + enclosing ring. Everything is even stroke weight so it cuts clean.

Palette. #1B3A4B (pre-dawn navy, the cart body and ring) and #E07A5F (terracotta, the wheels and any accent on the hatch). Cool sky versus warm coffee; the terracotta still feels like roasted beans, not coral decoration.

Font pairing. Wordmark in Space Grotesk medium (slightly wide, modern, the geometry of wheels and orbits). Optional descriptor in Lora italic — a quiet serif that adds the handmade cart feeling without going scripty or hard to read on a moving sign.

All three lock up as icon-plus-wordmark or icon-only. I would test them first as 16-pixel favicons and as 8 cm vinyl on the cart side; if a shape falls apart there, it doesn’t go further. The Cup-as-Sun version is the most flexible for merch; Bean Trajectory feels best as a stamp; Cart Halo is the one that tells people, from across the park, that this is a moving coffee stop.
