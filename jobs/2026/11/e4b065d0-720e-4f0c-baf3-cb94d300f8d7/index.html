The work, as delivered

The deliverable contains an eight-question survey focused on the customer's most recent coffee shop visit. Seven questions use a five-point satisfaction scale with a Not applicable option, and one asks for an open-text improvement suggestion. Each question includes interpretation guidance, followed by a short analysis guide.

  • Covers overall satisfaction, product quality, order accuracy, staff, wait time, atmosphere, and value.
  • Asks customers for one thing that could improve their next visit.
  • Recommends reporting response counts and the percentage satisfied or very satisfied.
  • Excludes Not applicable responses from satisfaction calculations.
  • Advises keeping wording and response options consistent for comparisons over time.
  • Suggests grouping comments into themes without treating vivid examples as proof of prevalence.

Coffee Shop Customer Satisfaction Survey

Thinking about your most recent visit, please answer the following. For Questions 1–7, choose one answer: Very dissatisfied, Dissatisfied, Neither satisfied nor dissatisfied, Satisfied, Very satisfied. Choose Not applicable if you didn’t experience that part of the visit.

#QuestionAnswer typeHow to read the results
1Overall, how satisfied were you with your visit?5-point satisfaction scaleUse this as your headline measure. Track the share who are satisfied or very satisfied, and watch for changes over time.
2How satisfied were you with the quality of your drink or food?5-point satisfaction scaleLow ratings point to a product-quality issue. Check comments or compare results by item, if you collect that detail elsewhere.
3How satisfied were you with the accuracy of your order?5-point satisfaction scaleA high share of dissatisfied responses may signal ordering, preparation, or handoff errors.
4How satisfied were you with the friendliness and helpfulness of the staff?5-point satisfaction scaleLook for patterns across visits or shifts before deciding whether service practices need attention.
5How satisfied were you with the time it took to receive your order?5-point satisfaction scaleLow ratings suggest wait times may be hurting the experience. Compare with busy periods or queue times where possible.
6How satisfied were you with the comfort and atmosphere of the shop?5-point satisfaction scaleUse low ratings to investigate factors such as noise, seating, cleanliness, or temperature.
7How satisfied were you with the value for money?5-point satisfaction scaleLow ratings may reflect prices, portion sizes, or perceived quality. Read this alongside the product-quality results.
8What is one thing we could do to improve your next visit?Open textGroup comments into recurring themes. Note both how often a theme appears and how strongly customers describe it.

Quick guide to analysis

  • Report the response count and the percentage selecting Satisfied or Very satisfied for each scaled question.
  • Keep Not applicable responses out of satisfaction calculations, and show the full response distribution when a result needs context.
  • Compare results over time using the same question wording and response options.
  • Use open-text comments to explain the scores and identify practical changes; a few vivid comments are useful examples, not proof of how common an issue is.
