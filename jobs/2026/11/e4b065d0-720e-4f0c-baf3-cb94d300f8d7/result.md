# Coffee Shop Customer Satisfaction Survey

**Thinking about your most recent visit, please answer the following.** For Questions 1–7, choose one answer: **Very dissatisfied, Dissatisfied, Neither satisfied nor dissatisfied, Satisfied, Very satisfied**. Choose **Not applicable** if you didn’t experience that part of the visit.

| # | Question | Answer type | How to read the results |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Overall, how satisfied were you with your visit? | 5-point satisfaction scale | Use this as your headline measure. Track the share who are satisfied or very satisfied, and watch for changes over time. |
| 2 | How satisfied were you with the quality of your drink or food? | 5-point satisfaction scale | Low ratings point to a product-quality issue. Check comments or compare results by item, if you collect that detail elsewhere. |
| 3 | How satisfied were you with the accuracy of your order? | 5-point satisfaction scale | A high share of dissatisfied responses may signal ordering, preparation, or handoff errors. |
| 4 | How satisfied were you with the friendliness and helpfulness of the staff? | 5-point satisfaction scale | Look for patterns across visits or shifts before deciding whether service practices need attention. |
| 5 | How satisfied were you with the time it took to receive your order? | 5-point satisfaction scale | Low ratings suggest wait times may be hurting the experience. Compare with busy periods or queue times where possible. |
| 6 | How satisfied were you with the comfort and atmosphere of the shop? | 5-point satisfaction scale | Use low ratings to investigate factors such as noise, seating, cleanliness, or temperature. |
| 7 | How satisfied were you with the value for money? | 5-point satisfaction scale | Low ratings may reflect prices, portion sizes, or perceived quality. Read this alongside the product-quality results. |
| 8 | What is one thing we could do to improve your next visit? | Open text | Group comments into recurring themes. Note both how often a theme appears and how strongly customers describe it. |

## Quick guide to analysis

- Report the response count and the percentage selecting **Satisfied** or **Very satisfied** for each scaled question.
- Keep **Not applicable** responses out of satisfaction calculations, and show the full response distribution when a result needs context.
- Compare results over time using the same question wording and response options.
- Use open-text comments to explain the scores and identify practical changes; a few vivid comments are useful examples, not proof of how common an issue is.
