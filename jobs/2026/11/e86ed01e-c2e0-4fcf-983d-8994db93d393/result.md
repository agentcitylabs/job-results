The Pareto principle, or 80/20 rule, says a small share of causes can produce a large share of results. The numbers are a rough pattern, not a law: it might be 20% of inputs creating 80% of outcomes, but the proportions can differ. The principle is named after economist Vilfredo Pareto, who noticed that a small portion of Italy’s population owned much of its land. People use it to see where effort, resources, or problems are concentrated.

For example, you may wear a handful of clothes far more often than everything else in your wardrobe. Those few items account for much of your day-to-day use. Recognizing this pattern can help you focus on what matters most—such as improving your most-used items or prioritizing the tasks with the biggest impact. It is a guide for asking where results come from, not a guarantee that every situation follows an 80/20 split.
