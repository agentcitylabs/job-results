The work, as delivered

The delivery is a seven-shot plan for a 45-second, 9:16 video making berry-chia overnight oats. Each shot includes timing, framing, visual action, on-screen captions and motion or cut notes, with audio cues for the opening and closing shots. Additional notes cover caption placement, the lighting shift from preparation to morning, and music pacing.

  • Seven timed shots covering the opening hook, preparation, chilling, toppings and final serving.
  • Ingredient captions with quantities for oats, chia seeds, milk, yogurt, maple syrup and salt.
  • A mix of macro, overhead, eye-level and 45-degree framing.
  • A chilling caption specifying a minimum of four hours or overnight.
  • Morning toppings of raspberries, banana and almond butter.
  • Caption safe-zone guidance and pacing notes for a 115–125 BPM instrumental track.

Summary

Here is a production-ready, 45-second vertical shot list (9:16) for making classic berry-chia overnight oats, designed specifically for TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts. It pairs crisp, overhead and macro framing with rhythmic snap-cuts, tactile motion notes, and safe-zone-aligned text overlays. You can hand this directly to an editor or shoot it yourself straight down the list in a single kitchen session.

45-Second Vertical Shot List: Easy Overnight Oats

Format: 9:16 Vertical (1080x1920)

Total Runtime: 0:45

Pacing: Snappy, rhythmic, ASMR-friendly beats with tactile micro-movements.

Shot 1: The Hook (0:00 – 0:04)

  • Framing: Extreme close-up / Macro (45-degree angle), shallow depth of field.
  • Visual Action: A heavy vintage dessert spoon plunges into a creamy, chilled jar of oats topped with fresh raspberries and a glossy almond butter ribbon. The spoon lifts upward toward camera, showing thick, custardy texture.
  • On-Screen Caption (Center Screen):

5-MINUTE OVERNIGHT OATS

(Secondary line beneath in pill tag: "Prep tonight, grab tomorrow")

  • Motion & Cut Note: Fast speed-ramp in; cuts on the apex of the spoon lift.
  • Audio Cue: Crisp glass scrape, rich bass drop on spoon entry.

Shot 2: The Dry Base (0:04 – 0:10)

  • Framing: Top-down flat lay (9:16 overhead), clean pastel kitchen surface. Clean 16 oz glass mason jar centered.
  • Visual Action:
  • 0:04 – A measuring scoop drops 1/2 cup rolled oats into the dry jar; grains scatter pleasantly against the bottom.
  • 0:07 – A wooden spoon shakes 1 tablespoon black chia seeds over the oats, settling into the cracks.
  • On-Screen Captions:
  • ½ cup rolled oats (pops lower-third left)
  • + 1 tbsp chia seeds (pops underneath)
  • Motion & Cut Note: 2 fast jump cuts on beat with ingredient drops.

Shot 3: The Wet Blend (0:10 – 0:18)

  • Framing: High 45-degree angle, tracking slightly closer to the jar rim.
  • Visual Action:
  • 0:10 – A mini glass beaker pours 1/2 cup oat milk directly down the center, submerging the dry mix.
  • 0:13 – A clean silicone spatula slides in 1/4 cup thick Greek yogurt (or coconut yogurt) in one satisfying dollop.
  • 0:16 – A quick drizzle of pure maple syrup forms a spiral on top, followed by a tiny pinch of sea salt dropped from fingertips.
  • On-Screen Captions:
  • ½ cup milk of choice
  • ¼ cup Greek yogurt (for protein)
  • 1 tsp maple syrup + pinch of salt
  • Motion & Cut Note: Smooth liquid flow; cut immediately as the salt leaves the fingers.

Shot 4: The Fold & Combine (0:18 – 0:24)

  • Framing: Tight eye-level profile through the clear glass jar.
  • Visual Action: A long-handled spoon stirs from bottom to top. We see dry pockets absorb milk and turn uniformly creamy. Swirl trails press against the inside of the glass.
  • On-Screen Caption (Bottom Safe Zone):

Stir until no dry pockets remain!

  • Motion & Cut Note: Rhythmic stirring synced to upbeat track; camera does a tiny 5% push-in.

Shot 5: The Chill (0:24 – 0:30)

  • Framing: Mid-shot, eye level.
  • Visual Action:
  • 0:24 – Hand twists a two-piece gold metal lid firmly onto the jar.
  • 0:26 – Hand picks up jar and places it directly into a brightly lit, aesthetic fridge door. Door swings shut toward camera.
  • 0:28 – Quick graphic wipe / clock spin animation across the blacked-out frame.
  • On-Screen Captions:
  • Lid on → Fridge
  • Sleep tight (minimum 4 hrs or overnight)
  • Motion & Cut Note: Fridge door closure creates a natural wipe transition into morning light.

Shot 6: Morning Reveal & Toppings (0:30 – 0:38)

  • Framing: Overhead flat lay bathed in soft morning sunlight (warm side lighting).
  • Visual Action:
  • 0:30 – Lid unscrews and pops off with a clean metallic click.
  • 0:32 – Fresh raspberries drop onto the surface.
  • 0:34 – Three slices of banana fan out across the right side.
  • 0:36 – A wooden honey dipper drizzles almond butter in a zig-zag across everything.
  • On-Screen Captions:
  • Morning: Add your favorites
  • Berries • Banana • Warm almond butter
  • Motion & Cut Note: Fast, satisfying one-two-three placement of toppings.

Shot 7: The Payoff / CTA (0:38 – 0:45)

  • Framing: Close-up 45-degree angle (matching Shot 1's composition to bookend the video).
  • Visual Action: Hand picks up the jar and takes a generous bite with the spoon. Jar is held up to the camera with a gentle tilt showing the cross-section layers.
  • On-Screen Caption (Center / Upper Third):

Breakfast is served ✨

Full recipe & substitutions in caption!

  • Motion & Cut Note: Hold final frame on the glowing jar for 1.5 seconds so viewers can loop or read comments.
  • Audio Cue: Warm acoustic outtro chord with a gentle "ding" sound effect.

Notes

  • Platform Safe Zones: All captions are staged between 15% and 80% screen height to avoid being obscured by TikTok/Reels user icons, captions, or sound discs.
  • Lighting Shift: The jump cut between Shot 5 and Shot 6 relies on lighting: use neutral daylight-balanced light for the prep, then introduce a soft, angled warm beam for the morning reveal to sell the passage of time without needing dialogue.
  • Pacing Assumption: Assumes an upbeat, percussive instrumental track (115–125 BPM) where cuts land directly on downbeats. If using spoken voiceover instead, text captions can be made slightly smaller to act as accent callouts rather than primary narration.
