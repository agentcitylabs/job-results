# Casual Puzzle Game — One-Page Launch Plan

**Assumption:** The game is a free-to-play mobile puzzle for iOS and Android, ready to launch in both stores. The first week is about finding the right players, spotting onboarding friction, and learning which puzzles and messages bring people back—not maximizing installs at any cost.

## Audience

**Primary:** Adults who enjoy short, satisfying brain breaks—commuters, casual players, and people who play in spare moments. They want clear rules, quick wins, and a gentle challenge without a steep learning curve.

**Secondary:** Regular puzzle fans looking for fresh levels and a reason to return each day.

Lead with the game’s distinct hook—its core puzzle mechanic, visual style, or daily challenge—rather than broad claims like “fun for everyone.”

## Three launch channels

1. **App Store and Google Play listings**  
   Use a clear title, readable puzzle-focused screenshots, a short gameplay video, and a description that explains the hook and first-play experience. Check that store links and analytics attribution work before launch.

2. **Short-form social video**  
   Post 10–20 second clips showing a real puzzle, a satisfying solve, or a tempting “can you spot it?” moment. End with a simple invitation to try the game. Prioritize the platforms where the team can consistently reply and post.

3. **Puzzle communities and small creators**  
   Share a playable link and a concise pitch with relevant puzzle groups and a handful of small creators whose audiences enjoy casual games. Invite honest play and feedback; don’t disguise promotion as a recommendation.

## First-week actions

- **Day 1 — Launch:** Publish both store listings. Test install links, tutorial flow, and event tracking on real devices. Post the strongest gameplay clip; respond to comments and store reviews.
- **Days 2–3 — Check the first-play experience:** Review where new players quit, especially during the tutorial and first few levels. Fix broken flows or confusing instructions quickly. Share a second clip featuring a different puzzle.
- **Days 4–5 — Bring in targeted players:** Contact selected puzzle communities and small creators with a direct, personal invitation. Share a daily challenge or a short “solve this” post to prompt replies.
- **Days 6–7 — Learn and adjust:** Compare channel traffic and player behavior. Rework the weakest screenshot, video opening, or tutorial step based on evidence. Thank early players publicly and note the most-requested improvements.

## Measurement

Track daily by **source, platform, and install cohort**:

- **Reach and intent:** video views, meaningful comments, community link clicks, and store-page visits.
- **Acquisition:** installs and store-page-to-install conversion.
- **First-play quality:** tutorial completion, first-level completion, time to first solve, and early quit points.
- **Return and reliability:** Day 1 retention, crashes, and store rating/review themes. Day 7 retention can be read for launch-day players once they reach day seven.

Use initial guardrails—not universal benchmarks—to guide investigation: **25%+ store conversion, 70%+ tutorial completion, 25%+ Day 1 retention, and 99.5%+ crash-free sessions.** If a metric falls short, inspect the player journey and cohort size before changing the game or shifting effort between channels.
