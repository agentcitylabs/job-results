### Saving Money on Groceries: A 6-Post Thread

**1/6**  
Before shopping, check your fridge, freezer, and pantry. Build meals around what you already have, then write a list for only the missing ingredients. The best grocery deal is the food you don’t buy twice.

**2/6**  
Plan 3–4 flexible meals, not seven rigid ones. Choose ingredients that can do double duty: roast chicken becomes sandwiches, rice bowls, or soup. Fewer unique ingredients means less waste and a smaller bill.

**3/6**  
Compare unit prices—not package prices. The larger size isn’t always cheaper, and store brands often cost less for nearly identical products. Take ten seconds to check the price per 100g or per item.

**4/6**  
Shop the perimeter carefully, but don’t ignore the freezer aisle. Frozen vegetables, fruit, and fish can be nutritious, last longer, and cost less than fresh. Buy only what you’ll realistically use.

**5/6**  
Give impulse buys a cooling-off period. Eat before shopping, use a basket instead of a cart, and wait 10 minutes before adding an unplanned item. If you still want it, check whether it fits your budget.

**6/6**  
At home, store food so you can see it. Freeze bread, herbs, and meat before they spoil, and keep a “use first” section in the fridge. Saving money is often less about buying cheaper—and more about wasting less.
