# Four-Week Plan: Launch a Local Bakery Newsletter

**Assumption:** The bakery is starting from scratch. The goal is to send its first newsletter by the end of Week 4, then continue monthly. Time estimates are hands-on work, not waiting time.

## Who could do it

Up to four people could cover the project; one person can take multiple roles:

1. **Bakery owner:** Approves the offer, budget, and final email.
2. **Newsletter lead:** A staff member who coordinates the work, collects content, and manages signups.
3. **Copywriter:** Writes the signup invitation and newsletter.
4. **Email specialist:** Sets up the email tool, designs the template, and handles testing and sending.

## Tasks, in Order

| # | When | Task and finished result | Rough time | Who could do it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Week 1 | **Define the newsletter.** Choose the audience, monthly frequency, and main goal—for example, bringing nearby customers back for seasonal specials. | 1 hour | Owner + newsletter lead |
| 2 | Week 1 | **Choose an email platform.** Compare cost, ease of use, signup forms, and expected list size; create the account. | 1 hour | Newsletter lead |
| 3 | Week 1 | **Set up sending essentials.** Configure the bakery’s sender name, reply-to inbox, domain authentication where needed, business address, and unsubscribe option. | 2 hours | Email specialist |
| 4 | Week 1 | **Plan the first issue.** Select one featured bake, a short behind-the-scenes story, and one clear action, such as “Preorder your weekend box.” Confirm prices, dates, stock limits, and how orders will work. | 1 hour | Newsletter lead + owner |
| 5 | Week 2 | **Build the signup form.** Explain what subscribers will receive and how often. Connect the form to the email list and test the signup and unsubscribe process. | 2 hours | Email specialist |
| 6 | Week 2 | **Create signup materials.** Write a short invitation; make a counter sign with a QR code; add the signup link to the bakery’s website and social profiles. | 2 hours | Copywriter + newsletter lead |
| 7 | Weeks 2–4 | **Invite subscribers.** Put the sign near checkout, brief staff on a friendly invitation, and share the link on social media. Only add people who have agreed to receive the newsletter. | 2 hours total, spread across three weeks | Newsletter lead |
| 8 | Week 2 | **Build a reusable email template.** Include the bakery’s logo, readable text, one main photo, a clear action button, opening hours, and footer details. Check the mobile layout. | 3 hours | Email specialist |
| 9 | Week 3 | **Gather content.** Take a few well-lit product photos, collect a baker’s quote, and confirm product details and links. | 2 hours | Newsletter lead |
| 10 | Week 3 | **Write the first issue.** Draft a subject line, preview text, short introduction, featured item, bakery story, and main call to action. | 2 hours | Copywriter |
| 11 | Week 3 | **Build and approve the email.** Add the copy and images to the template. The owner checks accuracy and approves the promotion. | 2 hours | Email specialist + owner |
| 12 | Early Week 4 | **Run a final test.** Send test emails, check phone and desktop views, click every link, test the ordering route, and verify the subscriber list contains only appropriate opt-ins. | 2 hours | Email specialist |
| 13 | Week 4, around Day 24 | **Send the first newsletter.** Schedule it when someone can monitor replies and orders. Confirm the campaign was sent successfully. | 1 hour | Newsletter lead |
| 14 | Week 4, around Day 28 | **Review and plan the next issue.** Record subscribers, delivery problems, clicks, replies, and attributable orders or redemptions. Pick one improvement and set next month’s topic and send date. | 1 hour | Newsletter lead + owner |

**Estimated total effort: 24 hours across four weeks.**

## Key Dependencies

- Set up the platform and test the signup form **before promoting signups**.
- Confirm the featured product and offer **before writing the email**.
- Get owner approval and complete testing **before sending**.

## What “Launched” Means

By the end of Week 4, the bakery has:
- A working signup route and a permission-based subscriber list.
- A reusable, mobile-friendly newsletter template.
- Its first newsletter sent.
- A named owner and a date for the next issue.

**Keep the first launch small:** one featured product, one clear action, and one good photo. A useful email sent on time beats a complicated campaign that never leaves the draft folder.
