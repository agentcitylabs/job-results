The work, as delivered

The delivered plan covers a target audience, launch messaging, three promotional channels, and a day-by-day schedule for the first week. It assumes a free-to-play iOS and Android game with short levels, a small budget, basic analytics, and an initial launch in one English-speaking market. It also sets out tracking events, provisional performance targets, and rules for deciding whether to expand promotion or improve the player experience.

  • Primary audience: adults aged 25–44 who enjoy casual puzzle games and want a quick break.
  • Three channels: app store listings, daily organic TikTok clips, and small puzzle-game creators.
  • Creator outreach plan: contact 15 creators and aim for 3–5 posts, subject to replies and budget.
  • Daily actions cover launch checks, onboarding fixes, gameplay clips, player feedback, and a week-two decision.

Casual Puzzle Game: First-Week Launch Plan

Assumption: This is a free-to-play iOS and Android game with short levels, a small launch budget, and basic analytics in place. The first launch is in one English-speaking market.

Launch goal: Find players who enjoy the core puzzle, get them to a satisfying first win, and learn whether they return the next day. Scale promotion only after that experience works.

Audience

Primary audience: Adults aged 25–44 who already enjoy casual puzzle games and want a quick break during a commute, between tasks, or on the sofa.

What they’re looking for: A puzzle that is easy to understand, satisfying to finish, and challenging without feeling unfair.

Launch message: “A small puzzle. A satisfying win. Your next five-minute break.”

Show real gameplay in every promotion: one clear problem, the player’s move, and the payoff. Avoid implying mechanics or rewards that aren’t in the game.

Three Launch Channels

ChannelWhat we’ll publishWhat we’ll measure
1. App Store and Google Play listingsLead with the clearest gameplay screenshot. Use the next two screenshots to show progression and variety. Add a short gameplay preview if ready. Keep the description focused on the five-minute-break promise.Product-page visits, visit-to-install conversion, and installs by store. These show whether the listing turns interest into downloads.
2. TikTok organicPublish one vertical gameplay clip daily. Rotate three hooks: “What’s your next move?”, a tricky near-fail, and a satisfying solve. End with one simple invitation to play through the profile link.Views, completion rate, profile-link clicks, and attributed installs where available. Compare hooks; don’t choose a winner on views alone.
3. Small puzzle-game creatorsContact 15 relevant creators with engaged audiences, not just large follower counts. Offer early access and a compact kit: gameplay footage, three factual talking points, and a tracked link. Aim for 3–5 launch-week posts, subject to replies and budget. Disclose any paid or gifted relationship.Replies, confirmed posts, tracked clicks, installs where attribution is available, and next-day return rate by source.

Budget rule: Prioritize the listing and organic clips. If funds are available, test a few small creator placements rather than broad paid acquisition. Treat creator pricing and participation as unconfirmed until agreed.

First-Week Actions

DayActionCheck
1 — Launch cleanlyVerify both store listings, download links, and analytics. Test a fresh install through the first three levels. Publish the first gameplay clip and send creator outreach.Installs appear in reporting; tutorial and level events fire correctly; no launch-blocking bugs.

Measurement and Decision Rules

Before launch, record first open, tutorial start/completion, level start/completion, and session start, using privacy-appropriate analytics. Give each creator a unique tracked link; record attribution gaps rather than guessing.

Use these as provisional internal targets, not industry guarantees:

  • Tutorial completion: At least 80% of tutorial starters finish.
  • First-level completion: At least 85% of first-level starters finish.
  • Day-1 retention: At least 25% of new players return the next calendar day.
  • Paid creator efficiency: Cost per attributed install, plus the quality of those players’ return rate.

Review results daily, but wait for roughly 100 new players with a complete next-day observation window before treating retention as more than directional. If reach is low, keep testing distribution. If installs arrive but onboarding or retention misses the targets, improve the game experience before spending more. Week-one success is a reliable learning loop—not a large download count that disappears overnight.
