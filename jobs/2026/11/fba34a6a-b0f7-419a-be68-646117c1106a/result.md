# One-Page Launch Plan: Casual Puzzle Game

**Assumption:** The game is free-to-play, available on iOS and Android, and has short, easy-to-learn levels. Replace the working title and creative examples with the game’s actual details.

## Audience

Focus on **casual mobile players who enjoy quick, satisfying brain breaks**—especially people who play in spare moments and like clear progress, daily challenges, and puzzles they can share. Aim creative at adults who already engage with puzzle or word games; keep the promise simple: *easy to start, satisfying to solve.*

## Three launch channels

1. **App Store and Google Play**
   - Make the store pages do the explaining: a benefit-led first line, readable screenshots, and a short gameplay video showing the core puzzle in action.
   - Include relevant search terms naturally in the title, subtitle, and description. Check that the icon and first screenshot are legible at phone size.

2. **Paid short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, or YouTube Shorts)**
   - Test three quick creative angles: a satisfying solve, a tricky puzzle with a “can you spot it?” hook, and a daily-play or streak message.
   - Show real gameplay immediately. Use captions, a clear install prompt, and separate results by creative and platform.

3. **Small puzzle-game creators and communities**
   - Offer a small group of relevant creators early access or a simple launch code/link.
   - Invite them to show an honest first-play reaction or solve a challenge. Share launch posts in appropriate puzzle communities only where promotion is welcome; contribute a puzzle or useful tip rather than dropping a bare ad.

## First-week actions

**Days 1–2: Make the launch measurable**
- Verify store links, analytics, crash reporting, and attribution.
- Track the path from **ad or post → store visit → install → first puzzle completed**.
- Publish the store pages and launch the first paid-video tests. Keep spend modest until install quality is clear.

**Days 3–4: Find the strongest hook**
- Compare creative by click-through rate, store-page conversion, and cost per install—not views alone.
- Check early player behavior: tutorial completion, first-level completion, and crashes.
- Pause clear underperformers; make one variation of the best-performing creative.

**Days 5–7: Improve and build momentum**
- Share creator content and a daily puzzle or challenge that gives players a reason to return and share.
- Review retention and session data by acquisition source. Shift remaining paid budget toward sources bringing engaged players, not just cheap installs.
- Fix confusing onboarding or technical issues before scaling. Capture player feedback and turn recurring friction points into a short update list.

## How to measure success

Use a simple daily dashboard, split by channel and creative:

- **Discovery:** impressions, video views, click-through rate.
- **Store performance:** store-page conversion rate and installs.
- **Player quality:** tutorial completion, first-puzzle completion, Day 1 retention, and crashes.
- **Efficiency:** cost per install, plus cost per player who completes the first puzzle.
- **Early engagement:** puzzles completed per player and return visits during the week.

Set numeric targets after the first 48 hours establish a baseline. Treat installs as the start of the funnel; the key launch signal is whether new players understand the game, finish a first puzzle, and come back.
