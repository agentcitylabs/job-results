The work, as delivered

Bora delivered a discovery call script for selling website maintenance to small restaurants. It includes an opening, five discovery questions and a close aimed at booking a follow-up demo or proposal call rather than making an immediate sale. The work also includes six objections with responses and notes for the caller.

  • Opening checks availability and introduces restaurant website maintenance.
  • Five questions cover current upkeep, frustrations, business impact, downtime risk and past maintenance spending.
  • Close proposes a site review and a specific 15-minute follow-up.
  • Six objection responses address budget, a family-built site, time, past service problems, information requests and pricing.
  • Placeholders allow tailoring names, site observations, customer concerns, meeting times and price ranges.

Discovery Call Script: Website Maintenance for Small Restaurants

Context: Outbound or inbound discovery call, 10-15 minutes, goal is to book a follow-up demo/proposal call, not to close on the spot.

Opening (0:00–1:00)

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company] — thanks for taking a few minutes. I know restaurant hours are brutal, so I'll keep this tight.

The reason for my call: we help restaurants like [Restaurant Name] keep their website actually working — menu's up to date, hours are correct, online ordering doesn't break on a Friday night. I saw [specific detail — e.g., "your lunch menu still shows last season's items" or "your site took a while to load on my phone"], and figured it was worth a quick conversation.

Is now an okay time for 10 minutes, or should I catch you between shifts?"

(Wait for confirmation. If they're busy, offer two specific time slots for a callback — don't leave it open-ended.)

The Five Questions

Q1 — Current state:

"Who handles your website right now — do you update it yourself, does someone on staff do it, or has it kind of been sitting untouched?"

(This tells you if there's a decision-maker bottleneck or if it's purely neglect.)

Q2 — Pain point:

"When's the last time you tried to change something on the site — a price, a holiday hour, a new dish — and it was harder than it should've been?"

(Get them talking about a real frustration. Let them vent a little; don't rush this.)

Q3 — Business impact:

"Do you track at all how many people check the site before calling or walking in? Even roughly — has anyone ever shown up saying 'your website said you were open' when you weren't?"

(Connects the website to lost covers/revenue, not just an abstract "it looks old" problem.)

Q4 — Priorities/stakes:

"If your site went down completely tomorrow — no menu, no hours, nothing loading — who would even notice, and how fast could it get fixed?"

(This surfaces risk tolerance and usually gets a slightly nervous laugh, which is good — it means they're picturing it.)

Q5 — Budget/decision reality:

"Have you ever paid someone to maintain the site on an ongoing basis, or has it always been more of a one-time build-it-and-leave-it thing?"

(Tells you if you're educating on a new concept or competing against a past vendor relationship.)

Close (final 2–3 minutes)

"Okay, this is really helpful. Based on what you're telling me — [recap their specific pain in one sentence, e.g., "your menu's been stale for months and nobody in-house has time to touch it"] — here's what I'd suggest:

I'll put together a quick look at your current site, flag the two or three things that'll have the biggest impact fastest, and show you what ongoing maintenance would actually look like month to month — no big commitment, just so you can see it.

Does [specific day/time] work for a 15-minute follow-up where I walk you through that? I'll send a calendar invite either way so it's on your radar."

(Lock a specific date/time before hanging up. Never end with "I'll follow up" — always "let's follow up on [date].")

Six Objections & Responses

1. "We don't have budget for this right now."

"Totally fair — most restaurants I talk to feel the same way about anything that isn't food or payroll. Can I ask: what's it costing you when the menu's wrong or the site's slow? Usually it's not a budget problem, it's that nobody's put a number on the leak yet. That's really what the next call is for — no commitment, just so you can see the actual number before deciding."

2. "Our nephew/cousin built the site, we're fine."

"That's great — a lot of our clients started that way too. The thing is, most of those builds work fine until someone needs a change fast, and your cousin's busy with his own life. We're not trying to replace that relationship, just make sure there's someone who can turn things around in hours, not whenever he's free. Worth a quick look either way?"

3. "We're too busy to deal with this right now."

"I hear that constantly, and honestly — that's the exact reason this is worth 15 minutes. The whole point is you stop having to deal with it. Right now it sounds like it's already taking your time, just unpredictably. Let's find a slower moment this week — even a Tuesday afternoon — and I'll make it fast."

4. "We already tried something like this and it didn't work out."

"What happened — was it more that they were slow to respond, or the changes just didn't make a difference?"

(Listen first, then:) "That's useful to know. A lot of the maintenance services out there are really just hosting with a support email nobody checks. I'd rather show you specifically how we're different than ask you to trust me on it — that's what the next call is for."

5. "Can you just send me some information instead?"

"I can, but honestly, every restaurant's site has different issues, so a generic PDF won't tell you much. What I'd rather do is spend 15 minutes looking specifically at your site and showing you the two or three things I'd fix first — that way whatever I send after actually applies to you. Can we grab that time?"

6. "How much does this cost?"

"Good question — and I don't want to throw a number at you without knowing what you actually need first, because pricing really depends on how much hands-on work your site requires. What I can tell you is most of our restaurant clients land between [price range] a month, and the next call is where we figure out exactly where you'd fall and why. Fair?"
